# Peter Lynch — The Six Categories of Stocks

> "Behind every stock is a company. Find out what it's doing."
> — Peter Lynch, *One Up On Wall Street* (1989)

Peter Lynch managed Fidelity's Magellan Fund from 1977 to 1990 — earning **29.2% average annual return** over 13 years, the best long-running mutual fund performance in history. He grew Magellan from $18 million to $14 billion.

His method is fundamentally different from technical traders. Lynch buys companies he understands, holds them for years, and lets compounding work. His book *One Up On Wall Street* is the bible of bottom-up qualitative analysis.

This file extracts his **6-category framework** and translates it for KLSE.

---

## Part 1 — Why the Categories Matter

Lynch discovered that lumping all stocks together ("growth stocks" or "value stocks") is too crude. Stocks behave differently because the **type of business** dictates the **expected return, holding period, and key risks**.

Match your trade type to the right category, or you'll:
- Sell a Fast Grower too early
- Hold a Cyclical past the peak
- Treat a Turnaround like a Stalwart
- Expect tenbagger returns from a Slow Grower

The 6 categories give you a framework to **set realistic expectations** for each holding.

## Part 3 — Category 1: Slow Growers

### Definition
Mature companies in mature industries, growing earnings at 2-4% per year. Reliable dividend payers; "widow-and-orphan" stocks.

### Characteristics
- Earnings predictable but not exciting
- Large, dominant market position
- High dividend yield (typically 4-7%)
- Heavy regulation often (utilities, banks)
- Low PE multiples (often 10-14×)

### KLSE Examples
- **TENAGA** — power utility, regulated returns
- **GAS MALAYSIA** — gas distribution
- **MAXIS** — mature telco
- **TM** — telecom infrastructure
- **REITs (KLCC, IGB)** — yield vehicles

### Trading Strategy
**This is NOT a trading category — it's a holding category.**

- Buy at deep value (PE < 12, yield > 5%)
- Hold for dividend + slow capital appreciation
- Sell only when dividend cut or business deteriorating
- Position sizing: can be larger (10-20% of portfolio) because of stability

### When to Buy Slow Growers
- Phase 1 (Recession) — defensive, yield refuge
- Phase 4 (Early Recession) — defensive rotation
- When yield spreads vs bonds are attractive
- After temporary issues (regulatory disputes, dividend rebases) when business is intact

### When to Sell
- Dividend cut announced
- Business model under structural threat
- Yield falls below historical average (overpriced)

## Part 4 — Category 2: Stalwarts

### Definition
Large, established companies growing earnings at 10-12% per year. The "blue chips" of the market — predictable, profitable, defensible.

### Characteristics
- Strong moats (brand, scale, network)
- Decade-long earnings growth track record
- Moderate PE (12-20×)
- Dividend payers but lower yield than slow growers (2-4%)
- Often international expansion potential

### KLSE Examples
- **MAYBANK** — largest bank, brand, scale
- **PBBANK** — best-in-class banking ROE
- **PCHEM** — petrochemicals scale
- **NESTLE** — consumer brand moat
- **F&N** — beverages brand moat
- **DLADY** — dairy brand
- **CARLSBG** — beer monopoly

### Trading Strategy
**Buy-and-hold with periodic trimming.**

- Buy on pullbacks to long-term support (EMA50 or 200)
- Hold for 1-3 year periods
- Trim 25-30% at +30-50% gain
- Re-enter on next pullback
- Position sizing: 5-10% of portfolio per stalwart

### When to Buy Stalwarts
- Phase 2 (Early Recovery) — they participate in the rally
- After market corrections that affect them temporarily
- When trading at lower PE than 5-year average
- When dividend yield > 3.5%

### When to Sell
- PE expanded to >25× (overpriced)
- Growth deceleration (3+ consecutive quarters of slowing EPS)
- Loss of competitive moat (new competitor, technology disruption)
- Better opportunity in Fast Growers during Phase 2/3

### The Stalwart Compound Math

Holding a stalwart at 12% EPS growth, with 3% dividend, with PE expansion of +1% per year:
**~16% total annual return** — compounded over 10 years = **341% total return**

This is the wealth-building category. Boring but powerful.

## Part 5 — Category 3: Fast Growers (The Tenbaggers)

### Definition
Companies growing earnings 20-25%+ per year. Lynch's favourite category and the source of his biggest wins. These are the stocks that can return 10× ("tenbaggers") or more.

### Characteristics
- Small-to-mid cap typically (RM500M to RM10B market cap)
- Niche product/service in growing market
- Earnings accelerating quarter over quarter
- High PE (often 20-40×) — but justified by growth
- Low dividend yield (reinvesting for growth)
- Often low institutional ownership early (room for funds to enter)

### KLSE Examples (Past and Present)
- **VITROX** — tech inspection equipment (tenbagger 2015-2021)
- **INARI** — RF semiconductor packaging
- **FRONTKN** — precision cleaning for semis
- **HEXTAR** — chemicals/agriculture
- **MR DIY** — retail expansion story
- **PETRONM** — refining margin expansion (cyclical-fast grower)

### The Lynch Tenbagger Criteria

A Fast Grower with tenbagger potential MUST have:

1. **Small company in a big industry** — room to grow
2. **Product or service that won't go out of fashion** — durable demand
3. **Repeatable formula** — can the success be replicated geographically?
4. **Strong unit economics** — profitable per store/customer/unit
5. **Low institutional ownership** (<30% of float) — funds haven't piled in yet
6. **Insider ownership ≥10%** — management eats own cooking
7. **Trading at "reasonable PE relative to growth"** — PEG < 1.5

When a stock checks 6-7 of these AND has a textbook chart, it's the highest-priority trade.

### Trading Strategy
**Active trader's favourite — and most rewarding.**

- Buy on VCP/breakout setups (use all of [04](04_VCP_Pattern_Playbook.md), [42](42_Momentum_Masters_Advanced_VCP.md))
- Hold through normal pullbacks (10-15%)
- Take partial profits at +20-25% (Minervini rule)
- Trail with EMA21 or EMA10 (advanced trader)
- Position sizing: 3-8% per position (higher risk = smaller initial size)

### When to Buy Fast Growers
- Phase 2 (Early Recovery) — peak phase for this category
- Phase 3 (Full Expansion early) — still strong
- After earnings beats with raised guidance
- When chart shows VCP near 52-week highs

### When to Sell
- EPS growth decelerates for 2 consecutive quarters
- Story changes (loss of major contract, regulatory issue)
- Chart breaks down on heavy volume
- PEG > 2.5 (overpriced for growth)
- Stage 3 industry signals

### The Fast Grower Risk

Fast Growers are high-reward AND high-risk. They can drop 30-50% on a single bad earnings report. Hence:
- Smaller position sizes
- Tighter stops
- Take profits on the way up (don't wait for the top)
- Have multiple Fast Growers (diversification across stories)

## Part 6 — Category 4: Cyclicals

### Definition
Companies whose earnings rise and fall with the economic cycle or commodity cycle. NOT growth stocks — they're "cycle stocks."

### Characteristics
- Earnings highly variable (can swing from RM5/share to -RM2/share)
- PE ratio MISLEADING:
  - Low PE at cycle PEAK = expensive (earnings about to fall)
  - High PE at cycle TROUGH = cheap (earnings about to rise)
- Strong correlation with specific external drivers (commodity prices, interest rates, GDP)
- Often dividend cuts during down-cycles

### KLSE Cyclical Sub-Categories

**Commodity cyclicals:**
- **KLK, IOI, SIME PLANTATION** — CPO price driven
- **PCHEM, LCT** — petrochemicals (oil price + global demand)
- **PETDAG, PETRONAS** — oil & gas
- **HEALYBOR, MAYBULK** — shipping/oil shipping rates

**Economic cyclicals:**
- **GAMUDA, IJM, SUNCON** — construction (infrastructure spend)
- **SIMEPROP, MAHSING, ECOWLD** — property (interest rate cycle)
- **AEON, MR DIY** — consumer discretionary (consumer cycle)

**Financial cyclicals:**
- Mid/small banks — credit cycle sensitive
- Insurance — interest rate + claims cycle
- Brokerages — market volume cycle

### Trading Strategy
**Buy at the BOTTOM of the cycle. Sell at the TOP.**

The key: identify WHERE in the cycle you are.

```
Cycle Bottom (BUY):
- PE high or undefined (losses)
- Sentiment terrible
- Commodity prices at multi-year lows
- Industry capex collapsed
- Dividend cuts done

Cycle Top (SELL):
- PE low (5-10×) — looks "cheap"
- Sentiment euphoric
- Commodity prices at multi-year highs
- New capacity coming online
- Analysts upgrading
```

**The Cyclical Trap**: Buying a low-PE cyclical at the TOP because it looks cheap. The PE is low BECAUSE earnings are peaking; they'll halve in the next year.

### When to Buy Cyclicals
- After 18-24 months of decline
- When the specific cycle driver (CPO, oil, rates) bottoms
- When industry capex drops sharply (sellers exhausted)
- Stovall Phase 1 → 2 transition (for economic cyclicals)
- Stovall Phase 2 → 3 transition (for commodity cyclicals)

### When to Sell
- Cycle driver hits multi-year high (CPO, oil, etc.)
- Industry capex announcements surge (incoming oversupply)
- Insider selling accelerates
- Analyst PT upgrades become unanimous

### Position Sizing
- Smaller positions (3-5%) due to high variance
- Be willing to hold 1-3 years from bottom to top
- Use options/warrants for leverage if available

## Part 7 — Category 5: Turnarounds

### Definition
Beaten-down companies showing signs of recovery. Usually after a crisis, scandal, restructuring, or industry collapse.

### Characteristics
- Recent history: 50-80% drawdown
- Restructuring underway (new CEO, divestitures, debt restructure)
- Earnings currently negative OR depressed
- Improving operational metrics (margins, working capital, cash flow)
- Sometimes near bankruptcy — high binary risk

### KLSE Turnaround Examples
- **Sime Darby** post-2017 restructuring
- **MUDA** (paper) post-debt restructuring
- **DRBHCOM** automotive restructuring
- **MAS** (failed turnaround — became cautionary tale)

### Trading Strategy
**High risk, high reward. Selective and patient.**

### The 5 Conditions for a Valid Turnaround Trade

ALL must be present:

1. **New management with track record** — credible CEO who has turned around before
2. **Clear strategic plan published** — specifics, not vague promises
3. **Asset sales / debt reduction underway** — visible progress on balance sheet
4. **Operating metrics improving** — gross margin, working capital, cash conversion
5. **First quarterly EPS beat** — proof the turnaround is real, not narrative

Without all 5 — too speculative. Skip.

### When to Buy Turnarounds
- After confirmed Q1 EPS beat (NOT before)
- When stock chart shows base formation (Stage 1 ending)
- When debt-to-EBITDA improves significantly
- When dividend reinstatement announced (signal of confidence)

### When to Sell
- Turnaround fails: any quarter of regression
- Stock has rallied 100%+ from lows (recovery priced in)
- Dividend cut again (story breaks)

### Position Sizing
- Very small (1-3%) — high binary risk
- Diversify across multiple turnaround candidates (most fail; 1-2 successes pay for the rest)

## Part 8 — Category 6: Asset Plays

### Definition
Companies with hidden assets on the balance sheet not reflected in the stock price. The market is pricing the operating business — but ignoring the property, cash, investments, or other assets.

### Common Hidden Assets
- **Land banks** undervalued at historical cost (often property developers)
- **Investment property** carried at cost but worth 5-10× more
- **Quoted investments** in other listed companies (cross-holdings)
- **Excess cash** beyond operating needs
- **Pension surpluses**
- **Tax loss carryforwards**
- **Intellectual property** not on balance sheet

### KLSE Asset Play Examples
- **OYL Industries** — quoted investments worth more than market cap
- **Sin Heng Chan** — land banks at historical cost
- **Some closed-end fund-like holdings** (NPC, BJTOTO, etc.)
- **MK Land, MRCB** — land bank plays
- **Family-controlled conglomerates** — often have hidden cross-holdings

### Trading Strategy
**Patient value with a catalyst.**

The trick: the market only "recognises" the value when a CATALYST forces it. Without a catalyst, the discount can persist for decades.

### Catalysts That Unlock Asset Value
- **Asset divestiture announcement** (selling at market price)
- **Spin-off or demerger** (separating businesses)
- **Privatisation offer** (MGO at premium to book)
- **Activist shareholder action** (forcing distribution)
- **REIT conversion** (for property-heavy companies)
- **Strategic stake by acquirer**

### When to Buy Asset Plays
- Price-to-NTA < 0.6 (40%+ discount to book)
- Recent catalyst announcement
- Insider buying signals
- After long period of underperformance (mean reversion potential)

### When to Sell
- Catalyst plays out (asset value recognised in price)
- Catalyst aborted (no follow-through after announcement)
- Better opportunities elsewhere
- After 2-3 years if no catalyst materialises (opportunity cost)

### Position Sizing
- 3-7% per position
- Often a "side bet" alongside main trades

## Part 9 — The Lynch Diversification Model

Lynch advocated owning stocks across multiple categories — but with specific weighting based on the market environment.

### The Lynch Balanced Portfolio (Adapted for KLSE)

| Market Phase | Slow Growers | Stalwarts | Fast Growers | Cyclicals | Turnarounds | Asset Plays | Cash |
|-------------|--------------|-----------|--------------|-----------|-------------|-------------|------|
| Phase 1 (Recession) | 25% | 25% | 10% | 5% | 0% | 5% | 30% |
| Phase 2 (Recovery) | 10% | 25% | 35% | 15% | 5% | 5% | 5% |
| Phase 3 (Expansion) | 5% | 25% | 25% | 30% | 5% | 5% | 5% |
| Phase 4 (Slowdown) | 20% | 25% | 15% | 5% | 0% | 5% | 30% |

### Position Counts

Lynch suggested no more than:
- 10-15 stocks in active trader portfolios
- 5-7 stocks for retail investors
- Higher concentration acceptable in highest-conviction categories

For KLSE retail trader: **6-10 active positions** is the sweet spot.

## Part 10 — The Lynch Research Process

Lynch was famous for "10-bagger" stocks that he found by observing the world. His method:

### Step 1: Observe Your Daily Life

What's in front of you that's growing?
- Which restaurants have queues every day?
- Which apps does everyone you know use?
- Which stores are expanding aggressively?
- Which products keep selling out?

### Step 2: Identify the Listed Company

If it's a private company, you can't trade it. But often:
- Restaurant chains → listed parent company
- Apps → listed parent company
- Products → listed manufacturer

### Step 3: Apply the 6-Category Framework

Once you identify a company:
- Which category? (Fast Grower most likely if you noticed growth)
- Does it have the tenbagger criteria? (Part 5)
- What's the market saying via the chart?

### Step 4: Do the Numbers

- Read 3 years of annual reports
- 4 quarters of quarterly reports
- Verify the growth story in the financials
- Run [29_Reading_Financial_Statements.md](29_Reading_Financial_Statements.md) checks
- Apply [35_Stock_Valuation_Methods.md](35_Stock_Valuation_Methods.md)

### Step 5: Set the Trade Plan

- Entry: VCP breakout, pullback to support, or accumulate over time?
- Stop: Where does the thesis fail?
- Target: What's the upside in 12-24 months?

### KLSE-Specific Lynch Examples to Watch

- **MR DIY** — observable nationwide store growth
- **Family Mart** under QL Resources — convenience store rollout
- **Mynews / 7-Eleven Malaysia** — competitive expansion
- **PARKSON** vs **AEON** — Malaysian retail consumption trends
- **Padini, Bonia, Cocoaland** — branded consumer items you see in malls
- **Domino's (parent: Berjaya Food)** — fast food expansion

## Part 11 — Lynch's Top 25 Rules (Distilled)

1. **Know what you own.** If you can't explain it in 2 minutes, don't own it.
2. **Look for boring or hated industries with great companies.**
3. **Small companies in big markets have the most room to grow.**
4. **Avoid "the next big thing" — buy "the proven thing growing big."**
5. **Insider buying = strong signal. Insider selling = noise (could be many reasons).**
6. **Strong balance sheet beats sexy story.**
7. **A boring company name often hides a great business.**
8. **Buy what you understand from your own life.**
9. **Be willing to hold a winner 3+ years.**
10. **Match the trade type to the stock category.**
11. **Never invest in companies you wouldn't buy on margin.**
12. **Avoid hot tips from "people in the know."**
13. **You don't need to predict the market to make money.**
14. **Time in the market beats timing the market.**
15. **The best stock to buy may be the one you already own.**
16. **A stock down 50% can go down another 50% — never average down on a falling knife.**
17. **Companies grow earnings 4 ways: cut costs, raise prices, expand into new markets, sell more products.**
18. **Avoid companies that diversify into unrelated businesses (diworsification).**
19. **The Magellan-class winners were boring (donuts, hotels, used cars) — not glamorous.**
20. **Earnings drive prices. Trace the earnings.**
21. **Be willing to be wrong. The market is wiser than the analyst.**
22. **The best time to sell is when the story changes.**
23. **6-10 positions is enough — quality over quantity.**
24. **Buy a wonderful company at a fair price, not a fair company at a wonderful price.**
25. **The biggest gains come from small companies that grow.**

## Part 12 — Lynch + Minervini = The Complete Trader

Lynch is bottom-up qualitative (the business). Minervini is top-down quantitative + technical (the trend).

Combining them gives you the strongest individual stock selection:

```
LYNCH FILTER (qualitative)
  → Category 3 (Fast Grower) identified
  → Tenbagger criteria met
  → Business is understandable, growing, defensible

MINERVINI FILTER (quantitative + technical)
  → CAN SLIM score 12+/14
  → Trend Template 7-8/8
  → VCP base in place
  → Volume profile correct
  → RS Rating > +10 vs KLCI

= STRONGEST POSSIBLE STOCK SETUP
  Take full position at the cheat entry
```

If a stock passes Lynch BUT fails Minervini → wait for the chart to align
If a stock passes Minervini BUT fails Lynch → check the business; if no clear story, smaller size

Both passing = trade with conviction.
