# Ray Dalio — Big Debt Crises & Liquidity Tides

> "Cash is trash. But cash is everything when the cycle turns. Knowing which side you're on is the game."
> — Adapted from Ray Dalio's commentary on liquidity cycles

Ray Dalio's *Principles for Navigating Big Debt Crises* (2018, free PDF at bridgewater.com) is the single most thorough study of credit cycles ever published. Dalio analysed 48 major debt crises over the past 100+ years and distilled them into a repeatable archetype.

This file extracts the actionable parts of Dalio's framework — credit cycle mechanics, central bank liquidity tools, and how to identify the inflection points — and applies them to KLSE trading.

---

## Part 1 — The Foundation: Why Credit, Not GDP, Drives Cycles

Most traders watch GDP growth, unemployment, inflation. Dalio's contention: **the credit cycle drives all of them, and runs ahead of all of them.**

```
Credit availability changes      → Investment & spending changes
Investment & spending changes    → GDP changes
GDP changes                      → Employment changes
Employment changes               → Inflation changes
```

By the time GDP reports a recession, the credit cycle peaked 6-18 months earlier. By the time GDP reports recovery, credit has been expanding for months.

**The implication for KLSE traders**: track credit indicators, not lagging economic data.

### What Credit Looks Like in Mechanical Terms

> "One person's spending is another person's income."

When credit expands:
- Bank lends RM100 to borrower
- Borrower spends RM100
- That RM100 becomes someone else's income
- That person deposits RM50 back in the bank
- Bank can now lend that RM50 again
- Cycle repeats

When credit contracts:
- Loans get called
- Spending drops
- Income drops
- Deposits drop
- Less capacity to lend
- Reverse cycle accelerates

This mechanical view explains why credit cycles are non-linear: they self-reinforce in both directions.

## Part 2 — The Two Cycles (Short and Long)

Dalio identifies two interlocking debt cycles operating simultaneously.

### Short-Term Debt Cycle (Business Cycle) — 5-8 years

```
Year 1-3:   Recovery — credit cheap, GDP recovering
Year 3-5:   Mid-cycle — strong growth, rising rates
Year 5-6:   Late-cycle — inflation worries, rates peak
Year 6-7:   Slowdown — credit tightens, GDP slows
Year 7-8:   Recession — credit contracts, GDP falls
Year 8+:    Recovery begins next cycle
```

Most traders only think in terms of this cycle — and that's the visible one.

### Long-Term Debt Cycle (Generational) — 50-100 years

```
Decade 1-4:  Productive credit growth — debt builds, but produces income
Decade 4-7:  Bubble phase — debt grows faster than income; "everyone borrows"
Decade 7-8:  Top phase — credit can't expand anymore (debt/income too high)
Decade 8-9:  Deleveraging — painful reduction of debt-to-income ratios
Decade 9-10: Reset — central banks print money / debt restructuring
```

We are currently late in the long-term cycle globally (since ~1971 USD-gold delink). This explains why:
- Interest rates have been historically low for 15+ years
- QE/money printing has become normal
- Each downturn requires more central bank intervention
- Debt-to-GDP ratios are at all-time highs

### Why This Matters for KLSE

The short-term cycle drives your tactical positioning. The long-term cycle determines the rules of the game (how much liquidity central banks can deploy, currency stability, inflation regime). Both matter.

## Part 3 — The 4 Phases of Leveraging / Deleveraging

Dalio breaks each cycle into 4 phases. Recognising the current phase is the master skill.

### Phase 1: Early Cycle (Productive Credit Growth) — Best Phase to Trade

**Macro state**:
- Debt growth slightly faster than GDP growth
- Credit financing real productive investment (factories, infrastructure, tech)
- Inflation low and stable
- Asset prices rising at moderate pace
- Central bank rates accommodative

**KLSE signals**:
- BNM holding low OPR
- Bank loan growth picking up (>5% YoY)
- USD/MYR stable
- Corporate capex announcements rising
- New IPOs healthy, not euphoric

**Trading action**: AGGRESSIVE. This is when momentum trading shines. Bucket A maximum exposure. See [DUAL_STYLE_PLAYBOOK.md](DUAL_STYLE_PLAYBOOK.md).

### Phase 2: Bubble Phase (Excess Credit Growth)

**Macro state**:
- Debt growing 2× as fast as income
- Credit financing speculation, not production
- Asset prices accelerating beyond fundamentals
- "This time is different" rhetoric appearing
- Margin debt, leverage, and exotic credit products surging
- Central bank starting to lean against the wind (raising rates)

**KLSE signals**:
- Penny stock activity exploding
- WhatsApp trading groups proliferating
- IPOs getting frothy (everything goes up on listing)
- Margin financing utilisation surging
- Foreign hot money piling in
- Loan-to-deposit ratios at high levels

**Trading action**: SELECTIVE. Bubble phase produces some of the biggest individual gains (parabolic moves) but ends violently. Tighten stops. Take profits earlier. Reduce position counts.

### Phase 3: Top Phase (Credit Saturated)

**Macro state**:
- Debt service costs > income growth — can't borrow more productively
- Central banks raising rates aggressively
- Asset prices plateauing then weakening
- Defaults starting at the margins
- Yield curve flattening or inverting

**KLSE signals**:
- KLCI failing to make new highs despite "good news"
- Distribution days clustering
- Foreign funds beginning to net sell
- Bank NPLs (non-performing loans) ticking up
- Margin debt at multi-year highs but stocks not advancing

**Trading action**: DEFENSIVE. Reduce Bucket A heavily. Build Bucket B (yield). Hold cash. Watch for the trigger event.

### Phase 4: Deleveraging Phase (Credit Contraction)

**Macro state**:
- Defaults cascading
- Asset prices falling
- Banks pulling credit even from healthy borrowers
- Unemployment rising
- Central banks emergency easing

**KLSE signals**:
- KLCI down 20%+ from peak
- Distribution days dominant
- Foreign selling sustained
- USD/MYR strengthening on flight to safety
- Bank loan growth turning negative
- IPO market frozen

**Trading action**: CAPITAL PRESERVATION. No Bucket A. Maximum Bucket B at deep discounts. Cash ready for the inflection.

### The 4 Deleveraging Outcomes

Within Phase 4, central bank policy determines the path:

```
Outcome A: Beautiful Deleveraging
  - Currency devaluation + monetary easing + debt restructuring + austerity
  - Best outcome: growth resumes within 2-3 years
  - Examples: US post-2008, post-COVID

Outcome B: Inflationary Depression
  - Excessive money printing without restructuring
  - Currency collapse, hyperinflation
  - Examples: Weimar Germany 1920s, Argentina, Venezuela

Outcome C: Deflationary Depression
  - Insufficient response, debt deflation spiral
  - Long stagnation, asset price collapse
  - Examples: 1930s USA, Japan 1990s

Outcome D: Slow Grind
  - Partial response, prolonged sub-trend growth
  - Asset prices range-bound for years
  - Examples: Europe 2010-2020, Japan ongoing
```

The KLSE 1997-99 Asian Financial Crisis was closer to Outcome C (deflationary), while the 2008 GFC was closer to Outcome A (beautiful). The COVID 2020 response was definitively Outcome A globally.

## Part 4 — Central Bank Mechanics (How the "Punchbowl" Works)

This is the part most traders don't understand mechanically. Central banks have specific tools that directly affect KLSE liquidity.

### Tool 1: Interest Rate Policy (Conventional)

```
When BNM CUTS the OPR (Overnight Policy Rate):
  → Borrowing cheaper across the economy
  → Existing debt service falls (more disposable income)
  → Banks earn less on lending → push for volume → loosen credit
  → Equity discount rates fall → stocks worth more (DCF math)
  → Bond yields fall → fixed-income less attractive → flow to equities
  → REITs benefit from cheaper debt and higher relative yield
  → CURRENCY weakens → exporters benefit, importers hurt

When BNM RAISES the OPR:
  → Reverse of all above
  → Compresses equity multiples
  → REITs pressured
  → Strengthens MYR (in isolation; capital flow direction also matters)
```

**KLSE example math**: OPR cut from 3.0% to 2.5% (50bps) typically:
- KLCI up 3-7% in following 3-6 months (historically)
- REIT prices up 5-15% (yield compression)
- Banks slightly down initially (NIM compression) then recover with loan growth

### Tool 2: Quantitative Easing (QE / Non-Conventional)

Central banks BUY assets (bonds, MBS) with newly created money. The mechanics:

```
Central bank creates RM100B → buys government bonds from banks
Banks now have RM100B in cash instead of bonds
Banks must do something with it:
  → Lend it (corporate, mortgage)
  → Buy other assets (equities, FX, other bonds)
  → Park it as excess reserves

Result: liquidity flood. Asset prices rise broadly.
```

BNM has not used full-scale QE historically, but the US Fed has run multiple QE programs since 2008. Their QE directly affects KLSE via:
- Foreign capital flows (Fed QE → MYR strengthens vs USD → foreign buying KLSE)
- Risk asset appetite globally
- Lower discount rates everywhere

### Tool 3: Forward Guidance

Central banks signal future moves explicitly:
- "Rates will stay low through 2026" → markets price it in immediately
- "We are considering a hike at the next meeting" → bonds fall, currency rallies

For KLSE: BNM Monetary Policy Statement (issued after every MPC meeting) is mandatory reading. The CHANGE in language signals direction.

### Tool 4: Reserve Requirements (SRR for BNM)

The statutory reserve ratio — what % of deposits banks must hold at BNM.

```
SRR cut from 3.0% to 2.0%:
  → Banks have more capacity to lend
  → Loose monetary conditions
  → Liquidity injection (often RM10-30B for KLSE banking system)
```

SRR changes are infrequent but powerful signals.

### Tool 5: Liquidity Facilities

Emergency liquidity provided to banks during stress (COVID-19, GFC). Signals:
- Recognition of stress
- Central bank "doing whatever it takes"
- Often coincides with market bottoms

## Part 5 — The Liquidity Tide Indicators (KLSE Dashboard)

Monitor these to identify where in the cycle you are.

### Tier 1 — Direct Liquidity Signals

| Indicator | Source | What It Tells You |
|-----------|--------|-------------------|
| BNM OPR | bnm.gov.my | Current monetary stance |
| BNM MPC statement | bnm.gov.my | Direction of next move |
| M1 / M3 money supply | BNM monthly statistics | Real liquidity in the system |
| Bank loan growth (YoY) | BNM | Credit expansion or contraction |
| Banking system LDR | BNM | Credit capacity (loose if <85%) |

### Tier 2 — Market Pricing of Liquidity

| Indicator | Source | What It Tells You |
|-----------|--------|-------------------|
| 10Y MGS yield | BNM bonds | Bond market expectation of future rates |
| 3M KLIBOR | BNM | Interbank lending rate |
| USD/MYR | investing.com | Foreign capital flow direction |
| Yield curve slope (10Y - 3Y) | BNM | Recession warning if inverted |

### Tier 3 — Global Liquidity (Spillover into KLSE)

| Indicator | Source | What It Tells You |
|-----------|--------|-------------------|
| US Fed Funds Rate | Federal Reserve | Global monetary policy anchor |
| US 10Y Treasury yield | Treasury.gov | Global discount rate proxy |
| US Dollar Index (DXY) | investing.com | Global USD strength affects EM flows |
| Federal Reserve balance sheet | fred.stlouisfed.org | Global liquidity supply |

### The "Punchbowl Check" Scorecard

Rate each indicator 0 (tightening), 1 (neutral), 2 (loosening):

```
Sum 12-16/16 → Liquidity FLOODING (Phase 1) — go AGGRESSIVE
Sum 8-11/16  → Liquidity NORMAL (Phase 1-2 transition) — standard
Sum 4-7/16   → Liquidity TIGHTENING (Phase 2-3) — selective
Sum 0-3/16   → Liquidity DRAINING (Phase 3-4) — defensive
```

## Part 6 — The Dalio Warning Signs (Pre-Crisis Checklist)

These are the specific warnings that precede major debt-driven downturns:

### Tier 1 — Direct Debt Warnings
- Total debt-to-GDP at multi-year high
- Debt servicing costs >25% of GDP
- New debt growing >2× nominal GDP growth
- Mortgage / consumer debt at record highs
- Junk bond spreads at multi-year lows (complacency)

### Tier 2 — Behavioural Warnings
- Speculative borrowing (margin debt) at extremes
- Foreign borrowing without hedging (currency mismatch)
- Banks easing covenants competitively
- "This time is different" appearing in mainstream press

### Tier 3 — Asset Price Warnings
- Stocks at PE >25× while earnings growth slowing
- Property prices growing faster than income for 5+ years
- Crypto / collectibles / luxury at all-time euphoria highs
- Multiple bubbles simultaneously

### Tier 4 — Macroeconomic Warnings
- Yield curve inverted for 3+ months
- Manufacturing PMI below 50 for 3+ months
- Consumer confidence rolling over from peak
- Central bank "behind the curve" rhetoric

When you see **8+ of these warnings**, you're in late Phase 2 / early Phase 3. **Reduce momentum exposure aggressively.**

When you see **15+**, Phase 3 is imminent. **Capital preservation mode.**

## Part 7 — KLSE Historical Cycle Studies

Understanding Dalio is easier with KLSE-specific examples:

### 1997-1998 Asian Financial Crisis
- **Phase 1-2 build-up**: 1993-1996 KLSE bubble (KLCI 1996 peak ~1271)
- **Trigger**: Thai baht devaluation July 1997
- **Phase 4 (deleveraging)**: KLCI fell from 1271 to 263 (-79%) by Sept 1998
- **Recovery**: Capital controls + ringgit peg + restructuring → Outcome A-like recovery
- **Bottom lesson**: When EVERYONE knew "it's not going to recover," that was the bottom

### 2008 Global Financial Crisis (KLSE impact)
- **Pre-crisis (Phase 2 bubble)**: KLCI peaked May 2008 at 1525
- **Trigger**: US subprime → global credit freeze
- **Phase 4**: KLCI fell to 836 (-45%) by Oct 2008
- **Recovery**: Massive global QE → Outcome A → KLCI recovered to 1525 by Q4 2009
- **Lesson**: Coordinated central bank action shortened the cycle dramatically

### 2020 COVID Crash
- **Pre-crisis**: KLCI moderate at 1611 Jan 2020
- **Trigger**: Pandemic shutdown March 2020
- **Phase 4**: KLCI fell to 1207 (-25%) by Mar 19, 2020
- **Recovery**: Fastest in history — KLCI back to pre-COVID by Aug 2020
- **Lesson**: When central bank response is overwhelming and IMMEDIATE, cycle compresses

### 2021 Glove Stock Mini-Cycle
- **Phase 1**: Mid-2020 glove boom on COVID demand
- **Phase 2**: TOPGLOV from RM5 to RM26 (Oct 2020)
- **Phase 3**: Late 2020 - early 2021 distribution
- **Phase 4**: 2021 collapse — TOPGLOV back to RM5 by Q3 2022
- **Lesson**: Single-stock and sector cycles follow the same arc as broad cycles

## Part 8 — Trading the Dalio Inflection Points

The biggest single-trade gains come at the FOUR major inflection points in each cycle:

### Inflection 1: Phase 4 → Phase 1 (Recession to Recovery) — BEST OPPORTUNITY

```
What to look for:
  ✓ Central bank emergency easing (rate cuts to near-zero)
  ✓ Banking system stabilising (loan loss provisions peak)
  ✓ KLCI undercutting and reversing on heavy volume
  ✓ USD/MYR strengthening signs ending
  ✓ Foreign net selling slowing

Action:
  - Begin building Bucket A momentum positions in starter sizes
  - Specifically: tech, banks, construction
  - Start aggressive REIT accumulation in Bucket B
```

### Inflection 2: Phase 1 → Phase 2 (Recovery to Bubble)

```
What to look for:
  ✓ Loan growth >8% YoY
  ✓ Margin debt rising faster than market
  ✓ IPO market heating up
  ✓ Property prices accelerating

Action:
  - Maintain Bucket A but rotate within: from growth to commodities
  - Tighten stops gradually
  - Begin moving from cyclical REITs to dividend cyclicals in Bucket B
```

### Inflection 3: Phase 2 → Phase 3 (Bubble to Top)

```
What to look for:
  ✓ Yield curve flat or inverted
  ✓ Central bank done hiking (or about to be)
  ✓ Distribution days clustering
  ✓ Breadth deteriorating (fewer stocks making new highs)
  ✓ "This time is different" everywhere

Action:
  - Aggressively reduce Bucket A
  - Take profits, raise stops
  - Begin building defensive Bucket B (utilities, staples, healthcare)
  - Keep cash dry
```

### Inflection 4: Phase 3 → Phase 4 (Top to Crash)

```
What to look for:
  ✓ KLCI breaks 50-day MA with heavy volume
  ✓ Bank loan growth slowing/negative
  ✓ Currency crisis or rate spike
  ✓ Foreign net selling persistent
  ✓ McClellan Oscillator deeply and persistently negative

Action:
  - Exit ALL Bucket A
  - Hold only defensive Bucket B
  - 30-50% cash for the bottom-fishing later
```

## Part 9 — The Dalio Mental Models

Beyond mechanics, three of Dalio's mental frameworks improve trading judgment:

### Mental Model 1: "Pain + Reflection = Progress"

When you lose, document it. Without reflection, losses repeat. With reflection, they become tuition for the next cycle.

For trading: every Phase 3 → 4 transition will teach the next generation of traders the same lessons. Those who studied previous cycles will be ready.

### Mental Model 2: "Idea Meritocracy"

Don't let emotional attachment to a thesis prevent updating it. The market doesn't care what you predicted — only what's happening now.

For trading: if you predicted Phase 1 but Phase 2 signals are appearing, ROTATE. Don't argue with the cycle to defend your earlier call.

### Mental Model 3: "Believability-Weighted Decision Making"

Weight inputs by track record. A central bank chairman's signal carries more weight than a YouTube trader's opinion. Your own past predictions should be weighted by your track record on similar predictions.

For trading: stop reading social media. Read BNM statements, IMF reports, central bank speeches. Higher-believability inputs.
