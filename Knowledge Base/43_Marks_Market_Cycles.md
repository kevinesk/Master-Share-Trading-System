# Howard Marks — Mastering the Market Cycle

> "You can't predict. You can prepare."
> — Howard Marks, *Mastering the Market Cycle* (2018)

Howard Marks is co-founder of Oaktree Capital ($170B+ AUM), one of the world's most respected distressed-debt investors. His memos to Oaktree clients (free at oaktreecapital.com) are required reading for serious traders.

His 2018 book *Mastering the Market Cycle* distils 50 years of cycle observation into one framework: **knowing where you are in the cycle is more important than predicting where it will go**.

This file translates Marks's framework into operational KLSE rules.

---

## Part 1 — The Core Insight: The Pendulum

Markets do not move in straight lines. They **oscillate** between two emotional extremes:

```
                              ____
                             /    \
                            /      \    ← EUPHORIA (overpriced)
                           /        \      Greed dominates
                          /          \     "This time is different"
                         /            \
                        /              \
        Mean ─────────/────────────────\────────── Mean
                    /                    \
                   /                      \
                  /                        \
                 /                          \   ← PANIC (underpriced)
                /                            \    Fear dominates
               /                              \   "Stocks are dead"
              /                                \
             /__________________________________\
```

### The Key Properties of the Pendulum

1. **It is almost never at the midpoint.** Most of the time, markets are leaning toward one extreme or the other.
2. **It always returns.** Mean reversion is the most reliable feature of markets. Extremes never persist forever.
3. **The further the swing, the bigger the return move.** Deep panic = explosive rally. Extreme euphoria = severe correction.
4. **Most participants ride the pendulum** rather than fight it — which means most participants buy near tops and sell near bottoms.

### The Mastery: Knowing Where You Are

Marks doesn't try to predict WHEN the swing will reverse. He asks: **"Where are we now?"** That alone is enough to size positions, set expectations, and tilt offense vs defense.

> "We may never know where we're going, but we'd better have a good idea where we are."

## Part 2 — The Three Cycles Marks Tracks

Marks identifies three interlinked cycles that combine to produce market action:

### Cycle 1: The Economic Cycle
The standard business cycle — GDP growth, employment, inflation, consumer demand. Cycles run 5-10 years typically.

### Cycle 2: The Credit Cycle
The expansion and contraction of credit availability. **This is the cycle Marks emphasises most.**

```
Easy credit phase                Tight credit phase
─────────────────                ──────────────────
Lenders eager to lend            Lenders demand premium
Loose covenants                  Tight covenants
Low yields                       High yields
Borrowers in control             Lenders in control
→ Asset prices RISE              → Asset prices FALL
```

### Cycle 3: The Psychological Cycle
The pendulum of investor sentiment — fear vs greed, optimism vs pessimism.

### How They Interact

```
Strong economy → Lenders confident → Easy credit → Asset prices rise
→ Greed feeds → Investors pile in → Prices overshoot → Mania
→ Some default → Lenders cautious → Credit tightens → Prices fall  
→ Fear feeds → Investors flee → Prices undershoot → Panic
→ Distress creates opportunity → Bargain hunters appear → Cycle restarts
```

**The most important practical fact**: Credit cycle peaks (and bursts) cause the biggest market dislocations. When you see lenders being undiscerning ("they'll lend to anyone"), the top is near. When lenders refuse to lend even to good borrowers, the bottom is near.

## Part 3 — The Marks Cycle Position Scale (1-10)

A practical way to grade where the market is right now:

| Position | Description | Typical Signs |
|----------|-------------|--------------|
| **1-2: Capitulation Low** | Pure panic, forced selling. | Widespread bankruptcy fear; quality stocks at 50%+ discounts; financial media saying "stocks are dead" |
| **3-4: Early Recovery** | Doubt remains; bargains everywhere. | Best risk-reward window; price below intrinsic value; institutions slowly buying |
| **5-6: Mid-Bull** | Recovery confirmed, conviction normal. | Most stocks near fair value; news cautiously positive; volume normal |
| **7-8: Late-Bull / Excess** | Confidence high; complacency building. | New highs frequent; analysts uniformly bullish; valuations stretched; "this time is different" appearing |
| **9-10: Mania / Top** | Pure euphoria; warnings ignored. | Taxi drivers giving tips; IPOs every week; valuations absurd; new investors entering en masse |

### How to Assess Your Position Score

Each Sunday, score the market on these 6 dimensions (each 1-10), then average:

```
1. Valuation     : PE multiples vs historical average
                   Below avg = 2-4 / Average = 5-6 / Above avg = 7-9 / Bubble = 10

2. Sentiment     : Survey data + media tone + retail participation
                   Fear = 2-3 / Neutral = 5-6 / Greed = 7-8 / Euphoria = 9-10

3. Credit        : How easy is credit? Bond spreads?
                   Tight = 2-3 / Normal = 5-6 / Loose = 7-8 / Reckless = 9-10

4. Behaviour     : What are people doing with risk?
                   Avoiding = 2-3 / Cautious = 5-6 / Embracing = 7-8 / Chasing = 9-10

5. New issuance  : IPOs, SPACs, leverage levels
                   Few = 2-3 / Normal = 5-6 / Many = 7-8 / Flood = 9-10

6. Media tone    : What are headlines saying?
                   Doom = 2-3 / Mixed = 5-6 / Bullish = 7-8 / Mania = 9-10

Average of 6 = Cycle Position Score
```

## Part 4 — Action Per Cycle Position

### Position 1-2 (Capitulation Low) — AGGRESSIVE OFFENSE

```
What to do:
- Add to high-quality stocks aggressively
- Use VCP/breakout setups at oversold conditions
- Build positions in Stage 1 sectors transitioning to Stage 2
- Override your normal "max positions" rule — load up

What NOT to do:
- Stay in cash waiting for "more clarity"
- Trade short-term (the opportunity is positioning, not trading)
```

KLSE example: Mid-2020 (COVID lows), early 2009. These are once-a-decade opportunities.

### Position 3-4 (Early Recovery) — STANDARD OFFENSE

```
What to do:
- Take full-size positions on textbook setups
- Hunt for "new leaders" emerging in Stage 2 sectors
- Pyramid winners aggressively
- Hold positions longer than usual

What NOT to do:
- Take profits too early ("it's already up 20%")
- Get scared by minor pullbacks
```

KLSE example: Late 2009-2010, mid 2020 to early 2021. Best returns of the cycle come here.

### Position 5-6 (Mid-Bull) — STANDARD EXECUTION

```
What to do:
- Trade your normal system
- Take A+ and A setups
- Maintain typical position sizing
- Stay diversified across 4-6 positions
```

This is most of the time. Don't be tempted to deviate.

### Position 7-8 (Late-Bull / Excess) — DEFENSIVE OFFENSE

```
What to do:
- Tighten stops aggressively
- Take partial profits more aggressively (1/3 at +20%)
- Reduce position count
- Increase cash buffer
- Only A+ setups; skip A and B

What NOT to do:
- Pile into "this time is different" stories
- Chase parabolic moves
- Add to losers (any loser at this stage = exit)
```

KLSE example: Late 2007, late 1996 (pre-Asian crisis). The signs were there but most ignored them.

### Position 9-10 (Mania) — CAPITAL PRESERVATION

```
What to do:
- STOP new entries
- Move all stops to break-even minimum
- Take profits on all but strongest positions
- Hold cash heavily (50%+ of portfolio in cash)
- Watch for credit cycle warnings (yield spike, defaults)

What NOT to do:
- Argue with momentum ("but it could go higher")
- Try to short the top
- Ignore the warning signs
```

KLSE example: Glove mania August 2020. Many small-caps in Q1 2007. The signs were screaming — but everyone wanted "just one more move."

## Part 5 — The Specific Warning Signs Marks Watches

These are the late-cycle red flags. When you see THREE or more, you're at 8+ on the scale:

### Credit Warnings
- Junk bond yields uncomfortably low (no compensation for risk)
- Covenant-lite loans dominating new issuance
- Loans being made to companies with negative cash flow
- Leverage multiples reaching new highs

### Sentiment Warnings
- "This time is different" appearing in mainstream financial media
- Retail investor surveys showing extreme bullishness (>70% bullish)
- Margin debt at record highs
- Cash levels at major funds at multi-year lows

### Valuation Warnings
- Multiple sectors at PE >25× (vs historical 15-18× average)
- Companies with no earnings trading at high multiples
- IPO market on fire — many "story stocks" with no profits
- Speculative assets (crypto, meme stocks) attracting attention

### Behavioural Warnings
- Taxi drivers, salon staff, family members asking about stocks
- "Buying the dip" rewarded immediately every time
- Bears being publicly humiliated
- Volatility persistently low (VIX < 15 for extended periods)

### KLSE-Specific Warnings
- Penny stock rallies dominating volume
- Bursa Marketplace search interest spiking
- Limit-up days clustering in small-caps
- WhatsApp trading group activity exploding
- New retail accounts at brokers surging month-over-month

## Part 6 — The Mistakes Cycle-Aware Traders Avoid

### Mistake 1: Confusing Cycle Position with Direction
Knowing you're at position 8 doesn't mean the market drops tomorrow. It can stay at 8 for months. **The cycle position adjusts posture, not timing.**

### Mistake 2: Permanently Bullish or Bearish
Some traders are perma-bulls (always long, no caution at tops). Some are perma-bears (always defensive, miss bull markets). Marks's framework demands you change posture with the cycle.

### Mistake 3: Predicting Instead of Preparing
You CAN'T know when the cycle turns. You CAN prepare so the turn doesn't ruin you. Hard stops, position sizing, and cash buffer are preparation. "I think it'll peak in Q3" is prediction — and prediction has a 50% hit rate.

### Mistake 4: Listening to Cycle-Blind Voices
Most TV pundits and brokerage research is cycle-agnostic — same recommendations at position 3 and position 8. Filter their advice through your cycle position assessment.

### Mistake 5: Excessive Confidence at Mid-Bull
Position 5-6 is dangerous because everything works. Returns become attributed to skill instead of cycle. Then position 8 comes, the easy trades vanish, and overconfident traders take large losses.

## Part 7 — The Marks Mental Model: Second-Level Thinking

Marks's other major contribution: most investors think first-level; mastery requires second-level.

### First-Level Thinking
- "This is a great company. I should buy it."
- "The economy is improving. Stocks will go up."
- "The chart looks strong. I'll buy."

### Second-Level Thinking
- "This is a great company — but is that already priced in? What does the consensus expect? What would surprise people?"
- "The economy is improving — but is the market already priced for improvement? Will the improvement exceed or fall short of expectations?"
- "The chart looks strong — but at what cycle position? Are we 6 (early move) or 8 (late move)?"

### How to Develop Second-Level Thinking

1. **Always ask "compared to what?"** — Is this expensive vs history, vs peers, vs growth?
2. **Identify consensus expectations** — News, analyst reports, social media sentiment
3. **Ask what would be a positive vs negative surprise** — Surprise drives moves, not absolute reality
4. **Consider cycle position context** — Same data means different things in different cycle phases
5. **Identify your edge** — If everyone knows this, your edge is zero

## Part 8 — KLSE Application: The Marks Workflow

### Sunday — Monthly Cycle Check (15 minutes)

```
1. Score the 6 dimensions (Part 3) for KLSE current state
2. Average to a 1-10 cycle position
3. Compare to last month's score
4. Identify any of the warning signs from Part 5
5. Determine market posture for the coming week:
   - 1-4: Aggressive offense
   - 5-6: Standard execution
   - 7-8: Defensive offense
   - 9-10: Capital preservation
```

### Daily — Apply Cycle Posture

The posture determines:
- Position sizing multiplier
- Maximum open positions
- Stop loss tightness
- Trade selectivity (A+ only vs all setups)

### When the Cycle Position Changes

A meaningful change is ≥2 points up or down within a quarter. When this happens:
- Re-read the 7 commandments (Part 6)
- Adjust your watchlist composition
- Reassess your open positions in the new context
