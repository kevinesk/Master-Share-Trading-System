# Trading Psychology — The Inner Edge

## Why Psychology is the #1 Edge

The system (Trend Template + VCP + Risk Management) gives you an edge of perhaps +1.5–2% per trade in expected value. But a single emotional decision can wipe out 10 trades of profits in one session.

> "The most important organ in trading is not the brain — it's the stomach."
> — Peter Lynch

Technical skill gets you to break-even. Psychology is what makes you consistently profitable.

---

## The 7 Deadly Cognitive Biases in Trading

### 1. Loss Aversion (The #1 Killer)
**What it is**: Losses hurt 2× more than equivalent gains feel good (Kahneman & Tversky).
**How it kills you**: You hold losers too long ("it'll come back") and cut winners too early.
**The fix**: Pre-define your stop loss BEFORE entry. Make it a rule, not a decision.

### 2. Confirmation Bias
**What it is**: You seek information that confirms what you already believe.
**How it kills you**: You bought MAYBANK and now only read bullish MAYBANK news.
**The fix**: After entering, actively look for bearish arguments. If you can't counter them, exit.

### 3. Recency Bias
**What it is**: You overweight recent events. After 3 losses, you fear every trade. After 3 wins, you oversize.
**How it kills you**: Position sizes become emotional, not systematic.
**The fix**: Fixed position sizing formula. Never deviate based on recent results.

### 4. FOMO (Fear of Missing Out)
**What it is**: Chasing a stock that has already moved 15% because it might go more.
**How it kills you**: You buy extended, get the pullback, panic-sell at a loss.
**The fix**: There is always another setup. Missing a trade costs you 0%. Chasing it can cost you 5–10%.

### 5. Sunk Cost Fallacy
**What it is**: "I've already lost 10%, I can't sell now."
**How it kills you**: A –10% loss becomes –30%, then –50%. The stock doesn't know what you paid.
**The fix**: Ask yourself: "Would I buy this stock at this price today?" If no → sell.

### 6. Overconfidence
**What it is**: After a winning streak, you believe you've "figured out the market."
**How it kills you**: You increase position size or skip rules — right before a losing streak.
**The fix**: Journal every trade. Your win rate will remind you that you're wrong 40–60% of the time.

### 7. Anchoring
**What it is**: You fixate on a price you paid or a price you remember.
**How it kills you**: "CIMB at RM7 is expensive, I remember when it was RM5." (ignoring fundamentals now).
**The fix**: Evaluate stocks based on current conditions, not historical prices.

## The Psychological Cycle of a Trade

```
Entry euphoria → Small profit → Complacency → Pullback → Hope
→ Deeper pullback → Denial → Panic → Stop hit → Relief
→ "I should have held" → Revenge trade → Repeat
```

**Break the cycle**:
1. Use rules, not emotions, at every step
2. Know your stop before you enter
3. After a stop-out, pause. Wait for the next valid setup.

## The 5-Step Pre-Trade Mental Checklist

Before placing ANY order, answer these:

1. **Is my analysis based on the chart, or on what I want to happen?**
   - If you're rationalising a trade, don't take it.

2. **Where is my stop loss? Is it on a level or just a percentage?**
   - Stops must be at a technical level (below the VCP low, below the handle low).

3. **What is my maximum loss in Ringgit on this trade?**
   - Know the number. If it makes you uncomfortable, reduce position size.

4. **Is the market (KLCI) in a condition where this trade makes sense?**
   - If KLCI is below EMA50, all your conviction in the setup is irrelevant.

5. **Am I trading because there's a valid setup, or because I'm bored/fear missing out?**
   - Boredom trades almost always lose. Only trade when criteria are met.

## Handling Losses — The Protocol

Every trader loses. The difference is **how you handle it**.

### After a single loss:
- Accept it as the cost of doing business (like a shop owner's rent)
- Do NOT immediately re-enter the same stock to "get it back"
- Review: Did you follow your system? If yes, the loss is fine. If no, fix the mistake.

### After 3 consecutive losses:
- Stop trading for the rest of the day
- Review your last 3 trades: Were they valid setups? Did you follow rules?
- If valid setups: market conditions may be wrong — check KLCI vs EMA50
- If you broke rules: identify the specific rule and write it on a sticky note on your monitor

### After –10% portfolio drawdown:
- Reduce position sizes by 50%
- Only take the highest-conviction setups (Trend Template score ≥ 7/8)
- Do not attempt to "make it back fast" — this guarantees further losses

### After –20% portfolio drawdown:
- Stop all trading for 2 full weeks
- Do not look at your portfolio
- Write a post-mortem: What went wrong systematically?
- Return with smaller position sizes and strict adherence to rules

## The Trading Journal — Your Most Valuable Tool

A journal separates professional traders from gamblers. Professionals track everything; gamblers rely on memory (which is biased).

### What to Record Every Trade

```
Date        : 2026-05-19
Stock       : CIMB (1023.KL)
Setup type  : VCP breakout — 3 contractions
Entry price : RM8.20
Stop loss   : RM7.65 (below VCP low)
Target      : RM9.84 (+20%)
Position    : 2,000 shares = RM16,400 (16.4% of portfolio)
Risk amount : RM1,100 (2.2% of portfolio)
R:R ratio   : 1:2.8
Market cond : KLCI above EMA50, KLCI RSI = 58 (healthy)
Thesis      : Banking sector leader, VCP formed over 6 weeks, vol contraction confirmed

Exit date   : 2026-05-28
Exit price  : RM9.20
P&L         : +RM2,000 (+12.2%)
What worked : Volume confirmed breakout; held EMA21 on first pullback
What failed : Sold too early — stock continued to RM10.50 (+28%)
Rule broken : None — followed system
Lesson      : After +20% gain, consider keeping 1/3 position with EMA21 trail
```

### Weekly Journal Review (Every Sunday)

Answer:
1. Win rate this week: _____%
2. Average winner vs average loser ratio: _____
3. Did I follow my rules on EVERY trade? If not, which rule and why?
4. What market condition was I trading in? Was my exposure appropriate?
5. What is my ONE area to improve next week?

## Building Trading Discipline

### The 3 Rules That Override Everything

1. **Never let a winner become a loser.** Move stop to breakeven after +10% gain.
2. **Never add to a losing position.** Averaging down amplifies mistakes.
3. **Never trade without a defined stop loss.** Hope is not a strategy.

### Daily Routine for Mental Performance

**Pre-market (30 min)**:
- Review macro (KLCI futures, USD/MYR)
- Identify the 2–3 best setups from your watchlist
- Write them down with entry, stop, and target

**During market**:
- Only look at your watchlist stocks, not random tickers
- Maximum 2 trades per day (decision fatigue increases errors)
- If you've had 2 losing trades, stop for the day

**Post-market (15 min)**:
- Record all trades in journal
- Rate your emotional control 1–10
- Identify if any rule was broken

## Mindset Principles from Elite Traders

| Principle | What It Means in Practice |
|-----------|--------------------------|
| **Detach from outcome** | You control the process, not the result of any single trade |
| **Think in probabilities** | "This setup wins 55% of the time" — not "will this trade win?" |
| **Embrace uncertainty** | A good trade can lose. A bad trade can win. Judge on process. |
| **Small losses are tuition** | Every stop-out teaches you something if you journal it |
| **The market is always right** | If price goes against you, do not argue with it |
| **Size controls emotion** | If you're emotional about a trade, you're too big. Reduce size. |

## Signs You Are Trading Well (Regardless of P&L)

- You cut losses without hesitation
- You feel no regret after following your rules, even on a loss
- You don't think about any individual trade after market close
- Your position sizes are consistent, not driven by conviction or fear
- You take breaks without feeling you're "missing money"
- You can explain every trade in one sentence: "I bought X at Y because of Z setup, with stop at W"
