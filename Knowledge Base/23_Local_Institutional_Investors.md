# Local Institutional Investors — How They Choose & Trade KLSE Stocks

## The Big 5 Malaysian Institutional Investors

| Institution | AUM (Est.) | Mandate | Listed? |
|-------------|-----------|---------|---------|
| **EPF / KWSP** | ~RM1.1 trillion | Retirement savings — preserve + grow | No |
| **PNB** | ~RM350 billion | Bumiputera wealth creation | No (owns listed companies) |
| **KWAP** | ~RM180 billion | Government pensioners | No |
| **Khazanah Nasional** | ~RM130 billion | Strategic national investments | No |

**Combined force**: These 5 institutions collectively own 40–60% of the free float of KLSE blue chip stocks. Their buying and selling MOVES prices. Tracking them is essential.

## 1. EPF (Employees Provident Fund / KWSP)

### Who They Are
Malaysia's compulsory retirement savings system. All employed Malaysians contribute 11% (employee) + 13% (employer) of monthly salary.

**AUM**: ~RM1.1 trillion (2025)
**Equity allocation**: ~35–40% in equities (~RM385–440 billion)
**KLSE equity allocation**: ~70% of equity portfolio in Malaysian stocks

### How EPF Chooses Stocks

**Investment philosophy**: Long-term value investing. They do NOT trade. They accumulate over months and years.

**Selection criteria**:
1. **Dividend yield**: EPF needs income to pay out its 6%+ annual dividend. Stocks must generate regular dividends.
2. **Earnings stability**: Prefers businesses with predictable, recurring earnings (banks, utilities, telcos)
3. **Market capitalisation**: Predominantly large-cap (>RM2B market cap) — they cannot enter/exit small caps without moving the price
4. **ESG compliance**: Increasing focus on Environmental, Social, Governance factors
5. **KLSE listing**: Must be listed on Bursa Malaysia

**EPF's largest holdings (publicly disclosed)**:
- MAYBANK (>10% ownership)
- CIMB, PBBANK, RHBBANK (5–10%)
- TENAGA (>15%)
- AXIATA, MAXIS
- IHH, DIALOG, MISC
- SIME, KLK

### When EPF Buys and Sells

**They buy**: On sustained weakness after quality hasn't changed (market corrections, sector rotations out). They see dips as opportunities, not threats.

**They sell**: Rarely. When they do, it's portfolio rebalancing (trimming overweight positions) or capital reallocation to better opportunities.

**How to track EPF movements**:
- **Bursa disclosures**: Any change in shareholding crossing 5%, 10%, 15% thresholds must be disclosed
- **Search on Bursa website**: Company announcements → "Substantial Shareholder Notice (Form 29A/29B)"
- **Look for**: "AMANAH SAHAM BUMIPUTERA" (EPF's nominee), "CITIGROUP NOMINEES" (foreign nominee for EPF)

### Trading Implications — Following EPF

**EPF buying = price floor**:
- When EPF increases stake in a stock during a market correction → strong signal the stock is undervalued
- Their buying provides a "floor" — the stock is unlikely to fall much below EPF's average cost

**EPF selling = caution**:
- If EPF reduces a major stake, investigate why. Has the business deteriorated?
- EPF selling is usually disclosed → gives you advance notice to reassess

**The EPF "Dividend Floor" Rule**:
If EPF owns >10% of a company and that company's dividend yield drops below 4%, EPF will reduce their position (they need yield). This creates a natural resistance to over-valuation in dividend stocks.

## 2. PNB (Permodalan Nasional Berhad)

### Who They Are
Government-linked investment fund focused on generating wealth for Bumiputera investors through unit trusts (ASB, ASN, ASW).

**AUM**: ~RM350 billion
**Popular product**: ASB (Amanah Saham Bumiputera) — pays 5–6% annual dividend

### How PNB Invests

PNB is the controlling shareholder of several major Malaysian companies:
- **SIME DARBY** (SIME, SDGUTHRIE) — plantation and industrial
- **MAY** (Malayan Banking subsidiary)
- **UMW Holdings**

**PNB's approach**: Strategic, long-term ownership. They don't just invest — they CONTROL the companies they own.

### Trading Implication
- When PNB's controlled companies restructure or divest non-core assets → Value unlock opportunity
- PNB-backed companies have implicit government support — lower risk of bankruptcy
- Watch for corporate exercises in PNB-controlled companies (mergers, privatisations)

---

## 3. KWAP (Kumpulan Wang Persaraan)

### Who They Are
Pension fund for Malaysian government civil servants (different from EPF which is for private sector).

**AUM**: ~RM180 billion
**Investment focus**: Capital preservation + income generation for pensioners

### Investment Strategy
- More conservative than EPF
- Higher allocation to bonds and fixed income
- Equity portion focused on dividend-paying large-caps (similar to EPF's holdings)

**Disclosed investments**: CIMB, MAYBANK, TENAGA, IHH, SUNWAY

### Trading Implication
Similar to EPF — their buying creates price floors in large-cap quality stocks. Less volatility in their movements compared to EPF.

### How to Use Broker Research

**Don't follow blindly** — but broker reports reveal:
1. What institutions are thinking about a stock
2. Consensus EPS estimates (useful for tracking earnings surprises)
3. Target prices (useful for understanding valuation ceiling)
4. Sector rotation calls from large banks

**The upgrade/downgrade cycle**:
- Stock upgraded by 2+ major brokers in same week → Institutional buying likely to follow → VCP setup potential
- Stock downgraded by major broker → Review your thesis; if fundamentals are weaker, exit

**Where to find broker reports** (free):
- i3investor.com → Stock page → Analysis reports
- Klsescreener.com → Stock page → Research
- Broker apps (Maybank IB app, CIMB Clicks, RHB Now) — clients get free research

## 5. Kenanga Investment Bank — Special Focus

### Why Kenanga Matters for KLSE Retail Traders

Kenanga is known for serving a large retail investor base and publishing accessible research.

**Their specialty**:
- Growth stock picks (often early on mid-cap winners)
- Regular sector rotation calls

**Track their "Buy" and "Upgrade" calls on mid-cap stocks** — these move prices on KLSE:
- When Kenanga initiates coverage with BUY → 2–5% single-day price surge common
- When Kenanga upgrades target price significantly → institutional attention follows

## How Institutions Actually Execute Trades (Market Microstructure)

### They Don't Buy All At Once

A fund wanting to buy 5 million MAYBANK shares (worth ~RM37.5M) cannot put a single market order. It would move the price against themselves.

**They use**:
1. **VWAP orders**: Buy throughout the day to get the day's average price
2. **Iceberg orders**: Show only 50,000 shares visible, with 1M hidden behind
3. **Dark pools**: Off-exchange transactions negotiated directly (not visible on Bursa orderbook)
4. **Accumulation over weeks**: Buy 100,000 shares/day for 50 days rather than 5M in one day

**What you see on the chart**:
- Institutional accumulation looks like: Price going sideways for weeks on declining volume, then breaking out
- This is exactly the Wyckoff accumulation phase (file 08) and VCP base formation
- The "dry-up" in volume during a base = institutions have finished accumulating and are waiting to mark up

### How to Spot Institutional Footprints

| Signal | What It Means |
|--------|--------------|
| High volume down day, price closes in upper half | Institutions absorbing selling (Stopping Volume) |
| Low volume up days in a base | Institutions not selling; holding |
| Sudden volume surge with price breakout | Institutions beginning to "mark up" |
| Large block trade announcements on Bursa | Institutional buyer/seller disclosed |
| Substantial shareholder form filed | New 5%+ institutional buyer entered |

## Tracking Institutional Flows — Practical Steps

### Daily (2 minutes)
1. Check Bursa Market Summary: Net foreign buy/sell value
2. Check i3investor → Market → Institutional Flow

### Weekly (10 minutes)
1. Check for any new Substantial Shareholder Notices (Form 29A) on Bursa for your watchlist stocks
2. Review broker upgrades/downgrades for your universe stocks
3. Check EPF or KWAP disclosed positions if recently updated

### Quarterly (30 minutes)
1. After each quarter's corporate announcements, check if institutional ownership changed significantly
2. New large institutional buyer = potential re-rating of the stock
3. Major institutional selling = investigate thesis before holding

## The Institutional Investor Cheat Sheet

| Institution | Owns What | Buy Trigger | Impact on Stock |
|-------------|----------|-------------|----------------|
| EPF | Banks, utilities, telcos | Market corrections | Price floor; accumulate with them |
| PNB | SIME, plantation, industrial | Corporate restructuring | Watch for value unlocks |
| KWAP | Similar to EPF | Portfolio rebalancing | Provides stability |
| Kenanga Research | Coverage = attention | Initiation or upgrade | 2–5% single-day surge |
| Foreign funds | KLCI components | DXY weakens, EM flows | 1–3 week sustained buying |
| Retail (local) | All stocks | News, tips, hot sectors | Short-term momentum |

**The hierarchy of who moves what**:
```
Foreign funds (DXY driven) → move large-cap KLCI stocks
EPF/KWAP → provide floors under blue chips
Kenanga/CIMB research → move mid-caps on upgrades
Retail investors → move small-caps, warrants, penny stocks
```

**Your edge as a retail trader**: You are MORE NIMBLE than any institution. You can buy 50,000 shares of a small-mid cap stock without moving the price. Institutions cannot. Use this advantage.
