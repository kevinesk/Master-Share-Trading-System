## Part 1 — The Cold Eye Philosophy

### The Three Pillars

Cold Eye's framework rests on three foundational principles:

#### Pillar 1: 价值投资 (Value Investing)
- Buy quality businesses below intrinsic value
- The MARGIN OF SAFETY is the key discipline
- Look for companies the market is undervaluing — not because they're bad, but because they're unloved

#### Pillar 2: 长期持有 (Long-Term Holding)
- The compounding period is decades, not months
- Quality businesses keep getting better
- Holding through volatility is the price of compounding

#### Pillar 3: 等待时机 (Waiting for the Right Time)
- Patience for entry: don't buy because you have cash; buy because the opportunity is right
- Patience to hold: don't sell because the stock has moved; sell because the thesis changes
- Cash is acceptable; activity for activity's sake is destructive

### Cold Eye's Background

- Born in modest circumstances in Malaya
- Became a financial journalist (deep market knowledge)
- Started investing seriously in his 30s with modest savings
- Compounded patiently over 4 decades
- Now widely regarded as the Malaysian Chinese investor's role model

His genius isn't in stock picking ability — it's in **process discipline and emotional control**. Average analysts with great discipline beat brilliant analysts with poor discipline.

## Part 2 — The Cold Eye Stock Selection Criteria

Cold Eye uses a SIMPLER quality filter than other masters — but applies it rigorously.

### The Cold Eye 8 Criteria

For any stock to enter his portfolio:

#### Criterion 1: Profitable for 10+ Consecutive Years
- No losses in the last decade
- Profitability proven across multiple cycles
- This filter alone eliminates 70%+ of KLSE stocks

#### Criterion 2: ROE 15%+ Sustained
- Higher than KC Chong's threshold (12%)
- Demonstrates excellent capital efficiency
- Below 15% = not interesting to Cold Eye

#### Criterion 3: Dividend Paid for 10+ Years
- Consistency matters more than yield
- Dividend cut = warning sign
- Growing dividend = strong positive

#### Criterion 4: Reasonable Debt Levels
- Net debt/equity < 0.5 preferred
- < 1.0 acceptable for capital-intensive sectors
- Above 1.0 = caution

#### Criterion 5: Operating Cash Flow > Net Profit
- Cash quality of earnings
- Cash conversion ≥ 1.0
- Below 0.8 = earnings quality concern

#### Criterion 6: Conservative Accounting
- No frequent revaluations of assets
- No reliance on one-off gains
- Conservative inventory and receivables policies
- Auditor consistency

#### Criterion 7: Understandable Business
- Cold Eye's rule: "If I can't explain it to my grandmother, I don't buy it"
- Simple business model
- Clear customer base
- Visible revenue source

#### Criterion 8: Reasonable Valuation
- PE typically < 20× for stalwarts
- PE < 15× for cyclicals
- Margin of safety: 20-30% below estimated intrinsic value

### The Cold Eye Decision Process

```
Pass all 8 criteria → Tier A holding (size 5-10%)
Pass 6-7 criteria   → Tier B holding (size 2-5%)
Pass 5 or fewer    → REJECT
```

## Part 4 — The Compounding Math (Cold Eye's Lifetime Approach)

Cold Eye's secret isn't picking 10× winners — it's COMPOUNDING modest winners over decades.

### The Compounding Calculation

```
Starting capital: RM 100,000
Annual return: 12% (achievable with quality KLSE stalwarts + dividends)

Year 10:  RM 310,000  (3.1× starting capital)
Year 20:  RM 964,000  (9.6× starting capital)
Year 30:  RM 2,995,000 (30× starting capital)
Year 40:  RM 9,305,000 (93× starting capital)
```

A modest 12% CAGR over 40 years turns RM100K into RM9.3M. That's life-changing wealth from disciplined compounding.

### The Drag of Activity

```
Same starting RM 100,000:

Approach A: Hold quality stalwarts at 12% CAGR
- Year 30: RM 2,995,000

Approach B: Active trading at 12% gross CAGR
- Less: Transaction costs (~1.5% per round trip × 10 trips/year = 15%/yr)
- Less: Mistakes (overtrading often produces lower gross too)
- Effective net: ~6% CAGR
- Year 30: RM 574,000

Difference: RM 2.4M less wealth from activity
```

Activity is the enemy of compounding. Cold Eye's discipline is fundamentally LOW-TURNOVER.

### The Dividend Reinvestment Multiplier

For long-term holders, dividends matter enormously:

```
Stock at 4% dividend yield + 8% capital growth = 12% total return

Over 30 years:
- Pure capital growth: 10× original
- With dividend reinvestment: 30× original (3× more)
```

KLSE compounders that pay 4-5% dividend yields with 6-10% growth are wealth-creation machines.

## Part 5 — The Cold Eye Holding Strategy

### When to Buy

```
Conditions ALL TRUE:
✓ Stock passes Cold Eye 8 criteria
✓ Macro environment not in late-cycle euphoria
✓ Valuation at or below historical average PE
✓ Or: Quality stock at unusual discount due to temporary issue
✓ You have the capital to deploy (not desperate for the move)
```

### When to Hold (Almost Always)

The DEFAULT is HOLD. Selling decisions need strong justification:
- Has the BUSINESS deteriorated?
- Has the MOAT weakened?
- Has the MANAGEMENT changed for the worse?
- Has VALUATION become extreme (PE > 30× for a 12% grower)?
- Is there a CLEAR superior alternative?

If none of these → hold.

### When to Sell

```
Sell ONLY when:
1. Business quality deteriorates (one of the 8 criteria fails)
2. Moat narrows or disappears (technology disruption, regulatory change)
3. Management quality degrades (CEO change, governance issues)
4. Valuation reaches extreme overvaluation (PE > 30× consistently)
5. Clear superior alternative emerges (rare)
6. Personal financial need (life event)

Do NOT sell because:
✗ Stock price went up (sells winners)
✗ Stock price went down (panic)
✗ Quarterly results miss slightly
✗ Macro environment looks scary
✗ Friends say to sell
✗ Boredom
```

### The Position Sizing

```
Highest conviction Tier A holdings: 8-12% each
Medium conviction Tier B holdings: 4-6% each
Total portfolio: 8-15 positions
Cash buffer: 10-20% (less than Tan Chong Koay's 30-40%)
```

Cold Eye runs higher equity allocation than Tan Chong Koay because his selections are higher-quality (lower drawdown risk).

## Part 6 — The Patience Framework (Cold Eye's Core Skill)

The single biggest distinguisher: Cold Eye's PATIENCE. He holds for decades when justified.

### The Three Types of Patience

#### Patience 1: Patience to Wait for Entry
- Don't buy because you have cash
- Wait for the RIGHT setup at the RIGHT price
- This can be months or years for the perfect entry
- Cash compounds at ~3% in money market funds — not zero

#### Patience 2: Patience to Hold Through Volatility
- A quality stock might drop 30% in a market crisis
- The BUSINESS hasn't changed; only the price
- Selling during the crash = locking in losses
- Holding through it = collecting the eventual recovery

#### Patience 3: Patience to Let Compounding Work
- The first 5 years feel slow
- Years 10-15: compounding becomes visible
- Years 20-30: wealth accumulation accelerates dramatically
- Years 40+: the patient investor is in the top 1%

### The Cold Eye Psychological Tools

How to cultivate patience:

#### Tool 1: 长远眼光 (Long-Term Vision)
- Visualise your portfolio in 30 years
- Each individual day's price move is meaningless
- Focus on the destination, not the journey's bumps

#### Tool 2: 不看股价 (Don't Watch the Price)
- For long-term holds, check prices monthly, not daily
- Quarterly results are the meaningful data points
- Daily watching breeds emotional decisions

#### Tool 3: 与企业一起成长 (Grow With the Business)
- Think of yourself as a co-owner, not a stockholder
- Care about the BUSINESS performance, not the daily quote
- Read annual reports as you would your own business plan

#### Tool 4: 隔离市场噪音 (Filter Market Noise)
- Don't watch CNBC/Bloomberg constantly
- Don't read forums except for specific research
- Don't get caught up in social media narratives
- Read only signal sources (annual reports, BNM, specific research)

#### Tool 5: 一定的现金 (Have a Cash Buffer)
- Having cash = not being forced to sell during crashes
- Knowing you have 1-2 years of expenses in cash = emotional stability
- Cash + quality stocks = unshakeable position

## Part 8 — Avoiding the Cold Eye Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Catching Falling Knives
A 50% discount on a fundamentally broken company is still a bad investment. Cold Eye buys only at DISCOUNT to FAIR VALUE of QUALITY companies — not arbitrary low prices.

### Mistake 2: Selling During Crises
The pain of crashes is real. But selling locks in losses. Quality stocks recover; many retail traders sell at the bottom and miss the recovery.

### Mistake 3: Diversification for Its Own Sake
Holding 30+ stocks doesn't diversify — it dilutes attention. Cold Eye holds 8-15 quality names with proper concentration.

### Mistake 4: Confusing Trading and Investing
Trading uses charts, volume, breakouts. Investing uses fundamentals, moats, decades. They are different activities. Cold Eye is an investor.

### Mistake 5: Listening to "Experts"
TV pundits, social media gurus, broker analysts — most are noise. Their incentives are misaligned with yours. Trust your own analysis.

### Mistake 6: Chasing Hot Sectors
The sector everyone's talking about is usually at the top. The sector nobody's talking about is often where opportunities lie.

### Mistake 7: Activity for Activity's Sake
Doing something feels productive. Often, doing nothing is the right answer. Cold Eye's biggest "trades" are the YEARS he didn't trade.

## Part 9 — Cold Eye's KLSE Wisdom

Specific KLSE observations from his 40+ years:

### Observation 1: The Government Matters
Malaysia is heavily government-influenced. Political risk is a real factor. But also, government-linked companies (GLCs) have advantages. Cold Eye respects this dynamic.

### Observation 2: The Family Conglomerates Are Different
Malaysian KLSE has many family-controlled groups. Cold Eye prefers groups where:
- Family has long history (3+ generations of business)
- Family treats minorities fairly (track record)
- Family interests align with shareholders
- No history of value extraction

### Observation 3: The Foreign Capital Effect
Foreign capital comes and goes based on EM sentiment. Their selling creates buying opportunities for patient local investors who understand the businesses.

### Observation 4: The Plantation Cycle
KLSE plantation stocks have ~5-7 year cycles. Cold Eye notes this is one of the few areas where TIMING the cycle adds significant value vs pure hold.

### Observation 5: The Banking Franchise
Malaysian banks are durable franchises. They're protected by regulation, scale, and switching costs. Long-term, they compound steadily. Cold Eye is a long-term bank investor.

### Observation 6: The Consumer Staples Brand Moat
Malaysian consumers are brand-loyal in food. NESTLE, F&N, CARLSBG have multi-decade brand moats. These compound steadily.

### Observation 7: The REIT Stability
M-REITs offer stable yield with capital appreciation potential. Good for patient yield-focused holders. Less for traders.

### Observation 8: The Tech Sector Volatility
Malaysian tech (semis equipment, OSAT) is highly cyclical. Cold Eye is selective here — only buys after multi-year underperformance, holds through cycles.

### Observation 9: The Property Cycle
Property is 5-8 year cycles. Cold Eye buys quality developers at cycle bottoms, sells at peaks. Doesn't buy-and-hold property indefinitely.

### Observation 10: The Hidden Value of Patience
The biggest KLSE winners over 30 years aren't the most exciting stocks. They're the boring, consistent compounders held for decades. PBBANK, NESTLE, MAXIS, TM — boring stocks made fortunes.
