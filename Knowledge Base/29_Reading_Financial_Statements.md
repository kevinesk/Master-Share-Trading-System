# Reading Financial Statements — The 15-Minute Analysis

## The 3 Financial Statements and What They Tell You

```
Income Statement → Is the business making money?
Balance Sheet   → Is the business financially sound?
Cash Flow       → Is the money real?
```

**The key insight**: Income statements can be manipulated with accounting entries. Cash flow cannot be as easily faked. Always validate profits with cash flow.

---

## Statement 1: Income Statement (P&L)

### The Key Lines to Read

```
Revenue (Sales)
− Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)
= Gross Profit ← Gross Margin = Gross Profit/Revenue
− Operating Expenses (Admin, Sales, R&D)
= EBIT (Earnings Before Interest & Tax) ← Operating Margin
− Interest Expense
= EBT (Earnings Before Tax)
− Tax
= Net Profit ← Net Margin = Net Profit/Revenue
```

### The 5 Questions From the Income Statement

**1. Is revenue growing?**
```
Revenue Growth = (Current Revenue − Prior Revenue) / Prior Revenue × 100
```
- ≥15% YoY growth = strong
- 5–15% = steady
- 0–5% = stagnant
- Negative = declining business

**2. Is gross margin stable or expanding?**
- Expanding margins = pricing power and/or cost efficiency
- Shrinking margins = cost pressure or competition eroding pricing
- Red flag: Revenue growing but gross margin shrinking = hollow growth

**3. Is core operating profit growing?**
- Strip out one-off items (gain on land disposal, insurance claims, tax incentives)
- Look at "recurring" or "normalised" earnings
- If net profit grew but EBIT didn't → the gain is from non-core items (unsustainable)

**4. What is the effective tax rate?**
- Malaysia standard corporate tax = 24%
- If effective tax rate is far below 24% → company has tax incentives (Pioneer Status, MSC status) → may expire
- If effective rate suddenly rises → earnings will fall even if EBIT is flat

**5. Are earnings per share (EPS) growing?**
```
EPS = Net Profit / Weighted Average Shares Outstanding
```
- Growing EPS with flat share count = real growth
- Growing EPS with shrinking share count (buybacks) = partially real growth
- Flat EPS with rising share count = dilution; earnings power stagnant

### KLSE-Specific P&L Nuances

| Industry | Watch For |
|---------|-----------|
| Banks | Net Interest Income (NIM) trend; Non-interest income (fees) |
| Plantation | CPO average selling price vs cost of production |
| Property | Revenue recognition timing (completed properties only) |
| Construction | Percentage of completion method distortions |
| REITs | Net Property Income (NPI); Distribution Per Unit (DPU) |

## Statement 2: Balance Sheet

### Key Sections

```
ASSETS                          LIABILITIES
Current Assets                  Current Liabilities
  Cash & equivalents              Short-term debt
  Receivables (debtors)           Payables (creditors)
  Inventory                       Other current liabilities
  Other current assets
Non-current Assets              Non-current Liabilities
  Property, Plant & Equipment     Long-term debt
  Intangibles (goodwill)          Deferred tax
  Investments                     Other long-term

                                SHAREHOLDERS' EQUITY
                                  Share capital + Retained earnings
```

### The 6 Key Balance Sheet Ratios

**1. Current Ratio** (Can the company pay short-term bills?)
```
Current Ratio = Current Assets / Current Liabilities
Target: >1.5 (at least RM1.50 of assets for every RM1 of bills)
Warning: <1.0 = potential liquidity crisis
```

**2. Net Debt / Equity** (How leveraged is the company?)
```
Net Debt = Total Debt − Cash
Net Gearing = Net Debt / Shareholders' Equity
Target: <0.5 (healthy); <1.0 (acceptable)
Red flag: >2.0 = highly leveraged; vulnerable to interest rate rises
```

**3. Receivables Days** (Are customers paying promptly?)
```
Receivables Days = (Receivables / Revenue) × 365
Target: Industry-specific; rising trend = warning
If receivables grow faster than revenue → customers not paying = revenue quality issue
```

**4. Inventory Days** (Is inventory piling up?)
```
Inventory Days = (Inventory / COGS) × 365
Rising inventory days = Products not selling well = future revenue/margin problem
```

**5. Return on Equity (ROE)** (How efficiently is shareholder money being used?)
```
ROE = Net Profit / Shareholders' Equity × 100
Target: ≥15% consistently
Great companies: MAXIS 26%, MAYBANK 11%, NESTLE 97%, PBBANK 12%
```

**6. Book Value / NTA per share** (What would you get if the company liquidated?)
```
NTA = (Total Assets − Intangibles − Total Liabilities) / Shares Outstanding
If Price/NTA < 1.0 → Stock is below book value (potential value play)
If Price/NTA > 5.0 → Very premium; requires exceptional growth justification
```

### Red Flags on the Balance Sheet

| Red Flag | What It Might Mean |
|---------|-------------------|
| Goodwill > 30% of total assets | Overpaid for acquisitions; impairment risk |
| Cash declining despite profits | Cash is going somewhere (debt repayment, capex, working capital) |
| Receivables growing faster than revenue | Booking revenue before it's collected |
| Debt rising while cash falling | Burning through liquidity |
| Negative equity | Company owes more than it owns → insolvency risk |
| Large "Other receivables" | Could be loans to related parties (red flag) |

## Statement 3: Cash Flow Statement (The Truth Test)

### The 3 Cash Flow Sections

```
1. Operating Cash Flow (OCF)
   Cash generated from running the business
   = Net Profit + Non-cash items (depreciation) − Working capital changes

2. Investing Cash Flow (ICF)
   Cash used for investments (capex, acquisitions)
   Negative is NORMAL (companies must invest to grow)
   Very negative = heavy capex or acquisitions (check if strategic)

3. Financing Cash Flow (FCF)
   Dividends paid, debt issued/repaid, shares issued/bought back
   Negative can mean paying dividends + repaying debt (positive signal)
```

### The Most Important Cash Flow Check

**Free Cash Flow (FCF)** = Cash the business truly generates after maintaining its assets:

```
FCF = Operating Cash Flow − Capital Expenditure (maintenance capex)
```

| FCF Status | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| FCF positive and growing | Business is self-funding; can pay dividends and grow |
| FCF positive but declining | Watch carefully |
| FCF negative occasionally | May be investing for growth (acceptable if deliberate) |
| FCF consistently negative | Business is burning cash; will need to raise capital |

### The Quality of Earnings Check

```
Cash Conversion = Operating Cash Flow / Net Profit
```

| Cash Conversion | Earnings Quality |
|----------------|-----------------|
| > 1.0 | Excellent — cash exceeds reported profit |
| 0.8 – 1.0 | Good |
| 0.5 – 0.8 | Moderate — investigate why |
| < 0.5 | Poor — profits may be partly fictitious |
| Negative while profit positive | **Major red flag** — investigate immediately |

**Example**:
- COMPANY A: Net Profit RM100M, Operating Cash Flow RM120M → Cash Conversion 1.2× → Excellent
- COMPANY B: Net Profit RM100M, Operating Cash Flow RM30M → Cash Conversion 0.3× → Earnings may not be real

## The 15-Minute Financial Statement Analysis Checklist

Do this for any stock you're considering holding for more than 2 weeks:

### Income Statement (5 minutes)
- [ ] Revenue growing ≥10% YoY?
- [ ] Gross margin stable or expanding?
- [ ] Net profit growing ≥10% YoY (strip out one-offs)?
- [ ] EPS growing (not just inflated by buybacks)?

### Balance Sheet (5 minutes)
- [ ] Net gearing < 1.0?
- [ ] Current ratio > 1.2?
- [ ] ROE > 12%?
- [ ] No large unexplained "other receivables" or "other payables"?
- [ ] Goodwill < 20% of total assets?

### Cash Flow (5 minutes)
- [ ] Operating cash flow positive?
- [ ] Free cash flow positive (OCF > capex)?
- [ ] Cash conversion > 0.7?
- [ ] Dividends funded by FCF (not debt)?

**Score**: If 10+ checks pass → High earnings quality → Fundamental reason to hold
If <7 checks pass → Investigate further before buying

## Reading KLSE Quarterly Reports (Speed Read Method)

KLSE companies report quarterly. Each report takes 5–10 minutes to review.

**What to read (in order)**:

1. **First page**: Quarter revenue and profit vs prior quarter and prior year same quarter
2. **MD&A** (Management Discussion & Analysis): Why did results change? Are reasons structural or one-off?
3. **Revenue breakdown by segment**: Which division is growing? Which is declining?
4. **Balance sheet change**: Did debt increase? Did cash decrease?
5. **Outlook / guidance**: What does management say about next quarter?

**Skip**: Detailed notes, related party transactions disclosure, all accounting policy disclosures (unless you have specific concerns).

## Spotting Earnings Manipulation (Red Flags)

| Technique | How Companies Do It | How to Spot It |
|-----------|-------------------|----------------|
| Revenue recognition fraud | Book revenue before actually earned | Receivables growing much faster than revenue |
| Expense timing | Delay recognising expenses | Profit high but cash flow low |
| Channel stuffing | Force products onto distributors | Inventory at distributors rising; returns increasing |
| Big bath accounting | Take huge write-offs in one bad year to boost future years | Sudden massive one-off losses |
| Related party transactions | Sell assets to related parties at inflated prices | Large gains from "other income"; related party disclosures in notes |
| Capitalising operating costs | Treat operating expenses as capital investments | Intangibles or "other assets" growing rapidly |

**The ultimate fraud detector**: Audit opinion. If the auditor issues a **qualified opinion** or **emphasis of matter** → treat all financial data with extreme scepticism.
