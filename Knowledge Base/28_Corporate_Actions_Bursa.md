# Corporate Actions on Bursa Malaysia

## What Are Corporate Actions?

Corporate actions are events initiated by a listed company that materially affect its shares — including price, number of shares outstanding, or shareholder rights.

**Why traders must know them**: Corporate actions can cause sudden, large price moves. Being caught unaware leads to unexpected losses or missed opportunities.

---

## 1. Dividends

### Types of Dividends on Bursa

| Type | Description | Payment |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Interim dividend** | Paid during the financial year | Cash (most common) |
| **Final dividend** | Paid at financial year end | Cash |
| **Special dividend** | One-off; often from asset sale | Cash |
| **Share dividend (scrip)** | Dividend paid in new shares | Shares |
| **Tax-exempt dividend** | Fully exempt from withholding tax | Most common in Malaysia |

### Key Dividend Dates

```
Announcement → Ex-Dividend Date → Entitlement Date → Payment Date
   (D)              (D + several weeks)     (D+1)         (D + 4-6 weeks)
```

| Date | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Announcement date** | Company declares the dividend |
| **Ex-dividend date (XD)** | Buy BEFORE this date to receive dividend; buy on or after = no dividend |
| **Entitlement date** | Record date; who holds shares receives dividend |
| **Payment date** | Cash deposited into your account |

### How Dividends Affect Price

On the XD date, the stock price is theoretically adjusted DOWN by the dividend amount.

**Example**: MAYBANK declares RM0.30 dividend, XD date is May 20.
- May 19 (last day to get dividend): MAYBANK closes at RM10.00
- May 20 (XD date): MAYBANK opens at theoretically RM9.70
- The RM0.30 drop is "given back" to you as the dividend

**Trading rule**: Don't buy a stock specifically for the dividend close to XD date. The price drops by roughly the same amount. Net benefit = near zero (before tax considerations).

**Real opportunity**: When a stock has a HIGH sustainable dividend yield AND is in Stage 2 uptrend → buy for capital appreciation + dividend income.

## 2. Rights Issues

### What It Is

The company issues NEW shares to existing shareholders at a DISCOUNT to the current market price, raising new capital.

**Example**:
- CIMB issues 1-for-5 rights at RM5.00 (discount from RM6.00 market price)
- If you own 5,000 shares, you have the RIGHT to buy 1,000 new shares at RM5.00

### How Rights Issues Affect Price

Rights issues are **dilutive** — they increase the number of shares, which reduces earnings per share.

**The theoretical ex-rights price (TERP)**:
```
TERP = (N × Current Price + Rights Price) / (N + 1)
Where N = ratio of old shares to new shares
```

**Example (1-for-5 rights at RM5.00, current price RM6.00)**:
```
TERP = (5 × RM6.00 + RM5.00) / (5 + 1) = (RM30 + RM5) / 6 = RM5.83
```

On the ex-rights date, the stock theoretically drops from RM6.00 to RM5.83.

### Trading Rights Issues

**Option 1: Exercise the rights** (buy the new shares at discount)
- If TERP > Rights price → exercising is profitable
- Cost: Pay the rights price; receive new shares at the adjusted price

**Option 2: Sell the rights** (on Bursa, rights are tradeable)
- Rights are listed as "COMPANY-OR" (Offer Rights) or "COMPANY-WR" (Warrants Rights)
- Value of 1 right ≈ Market Price − Rights Price (adjusted for ratio)
- If you don't want to invest more money, sell your rights for cash

**Option 3: Do nothing** (let rights lapse)
- Your existing shares are diluted; you lose value
- Never do this — always sell the rights if you don't want to exercise

### Red Flag vs Positive Rights Issue

| Signal | Implication |
|--------|------------|
| Rights issue to fund new capacity / acquisition | Potentially positive if well-priced |
| Rights issue to repay debt / avoid default | **Red flag** — company in financial stress |
| Rights at deep discount (>30% to market) | Usually forced — company desperate for cash |
| Rights at modest discount (<15%) | Standard capital raising; assess the purpose |

## 3. Bonus Issues (Free Shares)

### What It Is

Company distributes additional shares to existing shareholders FREE OF CHARGE from retained earnings or share premium.

**Example**: 1-for-2 bonus issue
- You own 2,000 shares at RM4.00 → Receive 1,000 additional shares FREE
- Post-bonus: 3,000 shares at theoretical price of RM2.67
- Total value: Same (RM8,000 either way)

**No economic value change** — bonus issues are accounting entries, not real wealth creation.

### Why Companies Do Bonus Issues

1. **Reduce share price** to improve liquidity (cheaper price = more retail buyers)
2. **Signal confidence** — management shows they have sufficient retained earnings
3. **Psychological effect** — retail investors perceive "more shares for same money" as a win

### Trading Bonus Issues

**Pre-announcement trade** (if you anticipate it):
- Companies with large accumulated retained earnings + low share price = bonus issue candidates
- Stocks often rally 5–15% on bonus announcement

**Post-announcement trade**:
- Price typically drops by the ratio amount on ex-date
- Some stocks recover quickly if the bonus brings the price into a "retail sweet spot" (RM1–3 range)
- High-quality stocks often trade HIGHER on an adjusted basis post-bonus (increased liquidity premium)

**KLSE rule**: A company doing a bonus issue is not giving you "free money." Assess whether the underlying business is good. If yes, the bonus improves liquidity and may attract more buyers → net positive.

## 4. Stock Splits

### What It Is

Similar to bonus issues: Company splits each share into multiple shares.

**Common splits on KLSE**: 1-into-2 (50 sen shares become 25 sen)

**Effect**: Share price halves, shares outstanding doubles, total value unchanged.

**Why it matters for traders**: Post-split, the stock is in the "retail accessible" price range → more buyers → typically better liquidity.

## 5. Warrants (Company Warrants — NOT Structured Warrants)

### What It Is

The company itself issues warrants giving holders the right to buy new company shares at a fixed exercise price over a defined period (typically 5 years).

**Format on Bursa**: `COMPANY-WA`, `COMPANY-WB`, `COMPANY-WC` etc.

**Key differences from structured warrants**:
| Feature | Company Warrant | Structured Warrant |
|---------|----------------|------------------|
| Issuer | The company itself | Investment bank |
| Dilutive? | YES — new shares issued on exercise | No — bank hedges separately |
| Duration | Usually 5 years | Usually 3–24 months |
| Exercise price | Fixed | Fixed |

### Trading Company Warrants

**Intrinsic value** = Stock price − Exercise price (if positive)
**Time value** = Premium for remaining time

**The leverage opportunity**:
- Stock at RM2.00, warrant exercise price RM1.50, 2 years to expiry
- Warrant trades at RM0.60 (intrinsic RM0.50 + time value RM0.10)
- If stock rises to RM2.50 (+25%), warrant rises to ~RM1.05 (+75%)

**Risk**: If stock falls below RM1.50 at expiry, the warrant is worthless.

## 6. MGO (Mandatory General Offer) / Privatisation

### What It Is

When a single party acquires ≥33% of a listed company, they are REQUIRED by Bursa rules to make an offer to buy ALL remaining shares.

**MGO offer price**: Typically at or slightly above recent market price; sometimes at a premium.

### Trading MGOs and Privatisations

**The strategy**:
1. When MGO is announced → stock price rises toward the offer price
2. Buy if the stock is trading below the offer price (risk arbitrage)
3. Profit = (Offer price − Purchase price) when the offer closes

**Risk**: Offer fails (rare but possible) → price falls back to pre-announcement levels.

**KLSE recent examples**:
- MAHB (Malaysia Airports) privatisation (2023) — offered at RM11.00; stock had been RM6–7
- MYEG privatisation attempt (various)
- UMW Holdings, DIGI, AXIATA/Celcom merger

**"Voluntary Delisting" / Privatisation signals to watch**:
- Major controlling shareholder already owns >50% and continues buying
- Company's market cap < book value (cheaper to privatise than stay listed)
- Controlling family has been buying shares consistently over 12+ months

## 7. Mergers & Acquisitions (M&A)

### Impact on Stock Prices

**Target company** (being acquired): Price typically jumps 20–40% on announcement
**Acquirer company**: Price often DROPS (concern about overpaying) or stays flat

### Trading M&A Events

**Pre-announcement speculation**:
- Unusual volume (2–5× average) in a stock with no news = potential leak / accumulation
- Bursa issues "UMA" (Unusual Market Activity) query if this occurs
- High risk — if no M&A materialises, price collapses

**Post-announcement risk arbitrage**:
- Buy target at current price; profit when deal closes at offer price
- Risk: Deal fails (regulatory block, financing issues)
- Spread: If offer is RM10 and stock trades at RM9.70 → 3.1% return over deal timeline
- Annualised return: 3.1% over 3 months = 12.4% annual → compare to risk of deal failure

## 8. Corporate Governance Red Flags

Watch for these in Bursa announcements — they often precede price declines:

| Red Flag | Meaning |
|----------|---------|
| Qualified audit opinion | Auditor found issues in the accounts |
| Auditor change (multiple times) | Usually trying to hide problems |
| Related-party transactions (large/undisclosed) | Money potentially flowing to insiders |
| Frequent extraordinary general meetings (EGMs) | Disputes or major restructuring |
| Director resignations (multiple, sudden) | Something is wrong internally |
| Unusual tax payments (suddenly very high/low) | Accounting irregularities |
| Negative cash flow despite reported profits | Profit may not be real (earnings quality) |
| PN17 status | Company in financial distress (Practice Note 17) |

**Rule**: If a stock you hold triggers 2+ of these red flags → exit immediately regardless of price.

| Time | What to Check |
|------|--------------|
| After earnings release | Any dividends declared? Rights issue announced? |
| Bursa announcements daily | New MGOs? M&A? EGMs? Privatisation talks? |
| Before XD date | Remove "ex-dividend" from price expectations |
| After bonus/split | Update your price records; new lot sizes |
| Before AGM season | Watch for management changes; read AGM minutes |

**Bursa announcement types to monitor**:
- Go to bursamalaysia.com → Company Announcements
- Filter by: "Dividend", "Rights Issue", "Bonus Issue", "General Offer", "Unusual Market Activity"
