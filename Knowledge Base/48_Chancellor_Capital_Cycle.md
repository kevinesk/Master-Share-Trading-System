# Edward Chancellor — The Capital Cycle (Supply-Side Macro)

> "High returns attract competition; competition destroys returns. Industries with low returns starve of capital; with reduced capacity, returns recover. This is the most predictable cycle in finance — yet most investors trade against it."
> — Edward Chancellor, *Capital Returns* (2015)

Most macro analysis focuses on DEMAND — GDP, consumer spending, inflation. Edward Chancellor flips this on its head with the **capital cycle** — focusing on SUPPLY. His book *Capital Returns* (a compilation of Marathon Asset Management's investor letters) documents how the capital cycle has predicted nearly every major sector boom and bust of the past 40 years.

For KLSE traders, the capital cycle explains why:
- Glove makers boomed and busted (2020-2022)
- Plantation stocks have multi-year cycles
- Shipping cycles last decades
- Oil & gas cycles correlate with capex, not demand
- Tech sub-sectors (memory, displays) follow predictable boom-bust patterns

This file translates the capital cycle for KLSE application.

## Part 1 — The Capital Cycle Mechanics

```
              ┌─────────────────────┐
              │ Stage 1: STARVATION │
              │ Industry beaten     │
              │ Low capacity        │
              │ Survivors profit    │
              └──────────┬──────────┘
                         │
                         ▼
              ┌─────────────────────┐
              │ Stage 2: BOOM       │
              │ High returns        │
              │ Stock prices rally  │
              │ New entrants arrive │
              └──────────┬──────────┘
                         │
                         ▼
              ┌─────────────────────┐
              │ Stage 3: CAPEX SURGE │
              │ Massive investment  │
              │ Capacity building   │
              │ Analysts bullish    │
              └──────────┬──────────┘
                         │
                         ▼
              ┌─────────────────────┐
              │ Stage 4: OVERCAPACITY│
              │ Supply > demand     │
              │ Prices crash        │
              │ Returns collapse    │
              └──────────┬──────────┘
                         │
                         ▼
              ┌─────────────────────┐
              │ Stage 5: DEFAULT    │
              │ Bankruptcies        │
              │ Capacity withdrawal │
              │ Industry consolidation│
              └──────────┬──────────┘
                         │
                         └──── back to Stage 1
```

### The Self-Correcting Mechanism

The cycle works because:
- High returns → CEOs and capital providers see opportunity
- Capital floods in → factories built, mines opened, ships ordered
- Supply expands → eventually overshoots demand
- Excess supply crashes prices and returns
- Capital flees → losses, bankruptcies
- Capacity shrinks → supply falls
- Survivors enjoy reduced competition → returns recover

**The timing**: Cycles take 5-15 years typically. Long-duration capital projects (mines, refineries, semis fabs, plantations) have the longest cycles.

## Part 2 — Why This Matters MORE Than Demand Analysis

### The Conventional Mistake

Standard analyst report: "We forecast 4% global oil demand growth in 2026. This is bullish for oil prices."

**Why this is wrong**: Demand growth is roughly stable and predictable (3-5% for oil, 2-3% for consumer goods, 5-15% for tech). It's NOT the variable that drives cycles.

The variable that drives cycles is **SUPPLY GROWTH** — and supply growth comes from capex decisions made 3-7 years earlier.

### The Chancellor Insight

```
What everyone watches: Demand changes (slow, predictable, marginal)
What actually drives cycles: SUPPLY changes (lumpy, volatile, decisive)
```

When industry capex doubles, supply will eventually double. When that supply comes online (often years later), prices crash regardless of demand.

The trader who tracks capex announcements is reading the future of the industry years ahead of the demand-watchers.

## Part 3 — The 5 Stages with KLSE Examples

### Stage 1: Starvation Phase (Best Entry Point)

**Characteristics**:
- Industry has been declining for 3-8 years
- Most companies are losing money
- Industry capex is at multi-year lows
- Margins are bad; layoffs and closures common
- Analysts are uniformly bearish
- M&A activity = bigger players acquiring distressed assets cheap
- Survivors are slowly improving

**KLSE Examples**:
- **Plantation 2018-2020**: CPO at multi-year lows (~RM2,000/tonne), capex at lows, M&A picking up
- **Oil & Gas 2016-2019**: Brent below $50, no new exploration capex, services companies bankrupt
- **Shipping (BDI) 2016-2019**: Baltic Dry Index near all-time lows, fleet scrapping
- **Memory chips (DRAM) 2019-2020**: Prices collapsed, capex frozen

**Action**: SELECTIVE accumulation of best-quality survivors. Not yet a momentum play; this is value/position trading.

### Stage 2: Boom Phase (Momentum Window)

**Characteristics**:
- Underlying demand starts to recover (slowly)
- Limited supply → prices begin rising
- Survivors' margins expand rapidly
- Stock prices rally sharply
- Sell-side analysts start upgrading
- Sector RS line turning positive
- Sector index breaks above MA200

**KLSE Examples**:
- **Plantation 2021**: CPO surged from RM2,200 to RM7,000 in 12 months
- **Oil & Gas 2021-2022**: Brent from $40 to $120, oil services rally
- **Tech / Semiconductors 2020-2021**: Chip shortage → prices up → INARI, VITROX boomed
- **Gloves 2020 (extreme)**: COVID demand + tight supply → TOPGLOV 5× in 6 months

**Action**: This is where Bucket A (momentum) is deployed. VCP breakouts have highest hit rate here. Full positions, cheat entries, ride the cycle.

### Stage 3: Capex Surge Phase (Warning Phase)

**Characteristics**:
- Companies report record profits
- Massive capex announcements (new factories, mines, fields, plantations)
- New entrants joining the industry
- IPOs in the sector frequent
- Optimism universal
- Valuations stretched

**KLSE Examples**:
- **Gloves Q4 2020 - Q1 2021**: Massive capex expansion announcements (TOPGLOV adding 50% capacity, HARTA expansion)
- **Plantation 2022**: Major capex on new estates
- **Oil & Gas 2014**: Petronas announcing massive deepwater capex right before crash
- **Semis equipment 2022**: Order books "stretching out 18 months"

**The Capex Indicator**: When industry capex is ≥2× the industry's previous cycle average → red flag. Overcapacity is being built. The bust is 18-36 months away.

**Action**: REDUCE positions despite continued price strength. Take profits aggressively. Tighten stops. The chart is still working, but the FUTURE is being broken.

### Stage 4: Overcapacity Phase (Top and Decline)

**Characteristics**:
- New capacity coming online en masse
- Inventory starts building
- Pricing power lost
- Margins peak then begin compressing
- Analysts start downgrading "due to short-term inventory issues"
- Sector RS line turning negative
- Sector index losing leadership

**KLSE Examples**:
- **Gloves H2 2021 - 2022**: Glove ASPs collapsed from USD80/1000 to USD20/1000 as capacity surged
- **Plantation H2 2022 - 2023**: CPO retraced from RM7,000 to RM3,500 as supply normalised
- **Oil & Gas mid-2015 to 2016**: Brent crashed from $115 to $26 — capex still online from 2014 announcements

**Action**: EXIT all positions in the sector. The capex tsunami is just beginning. This is NOT the buying dip — it's the start of a multi-year decline.

### Stage 5: Default Phase (Capitulation)

**Characteristics**:
- Margins negative for many companies
- Defaults and bankruptcies
- Capex cuts to near zero
- Stocks down 70-90% from peaks
- Analysts uniformly bearish
- "This sector is uninvestable" sentiment

**KLSE Examples**:
- **Gloves 2022**: SUPERMX, TOPGLOV down 80%+ from 2020 peaks
- **Oil services 2015-2016**: SAPNRG, BUMI ARMADA — massive write-downs, restructurings
- **Shipping 2016**: Maersk LNG, multiple bankruptcies globally

**Action**: STILL avoid. Wait for the capex-cut signal. The cycle returns to Stage 1 only after sufficient capacity has been removed.

## Part 4 — The Capital Cycle Indicators (KLSE Dashboard)

### Direct Capex Indicators

| Indicator | Where to Find | Signal |
|-----------|--------------|--------|
| Industry capex YoY growth | Company quarterly reports, industry reports | >50% YoY = warning; <0% YoY = opportunity |
| New plant / facility announcements | Bursa announcements | Surge = late-cycle; drought = early-cycle |
| Industry M&A activity | Bursa, business press | Defensive M&A (consolidation) = Stage 1; aggressive M&A (expansion) = Stage 3 |
| IPO pipeline in sector | Bursa filings, IPO trackers | Heavy IPO = late-cycle |
| Insider activity | Bursa substantial shareholder | Insider selling at peaks; insider buying at troughs |

### Margin and Return Indicators

| Indicator | Source | Signal |
|-----------|--------|--------|
| Gross margin trend (5-year) | Company financials | Expanding for 2+ years = late-Stage 2; compressing = Stage 4 |
| ROIC vs cost of capital | Company financials | ROIC > WACC = good; ROIC peaking = warning |
| Industry capacity utilisation | Industry reports | >90% = late-cycle; <70% = Stage 5 |
| Inventory days | Company balance sheets | Rising = Stage 3-4; falling = Stage 1-2 |

### Behavioural / Sentiment Indicators

| Indicator | Source | Signal |
|-----------|--------|--------|
| Sell-side coverage | Broker reports | Heavy coverage = late-cycle; minimal = early |
| Conference attendance | Industry conferences | Big crowds = late-cycle |
| Trade press optimism | Industry publications | Universal bullishness = Stage 3 |
| Job postings in sector | LinkedIn, recruiter reports | Surge = Stage 3 hiring; freeze = Stage 5 |

## Part 6 — The Glove Cycle 2020-2024: A Perfect Capital Cycle Study

The most clear-cut recent KLSE capital cycle. Trace it through the stages:

### Pre-2020: Stage 1 (Starvation)
- 2017-2019: Industry oversupplied; ASPs (Average Selling Prices) ~USD20/1000 pieces
- Glove makers' margins compressed
- Capex announcements minimal
- TOPGLOV trading RM4-5, HARTA RM5-6 — boring

### Q1-Q2 2020: Stage 2 (Boom Begins)
- COVID-19 → demand surge for medical gloves
- ASPs jumping from USD25 to USD60+ within months
- Margins exploded (gross profit margins 50%+)
- Stocks rallied 3-5× in 4 months
- This was the moment for momentum traders

### Q3-Q4 2020: Stage 2-3 Transition (Peak Boom + Capex Surge)
- ASPs above USD80/1000
- TOPGLOV: RM5 → RM26 (520% return)
- Companies announcing MASSIVE capex (+50% capacity expansion at TOPGLOV)
- New entrants flooding in (lots of "new glove company" IPOs across Asia)
- Universal analyst optimism
- **The warning was clear**: industry capex doubling = bust coming

### Q1-Q2 2021: Stage 3 (Capex Surge)
- ASPs starting to roll over but stock prices still high
- More capacity coming online
- TOPGLOV announced AUSTRALIAN listing — peak hubris signal
- Insider selling at TOPGLOV picking up
- **This was the EXIT signal for capital cycle traders**

### Q3 2021 - 2022: Stage 4 (Overcapacity)
- Massive new capacity online
- ASPs collapsed from USD80 to USD20 in 18 months
- Stocks down 70-80%:
  - TOPGLOV: RM26 → RM3 (-88%)
  - HARTA: RM22 → RM3.50 (-84%)
- "Inventory issues" became the analyst refrain

### 2023-2024: Stage 5 (Default + Consolidation)
- Industry consolidation begins
- Capacity being mothballed
- Some smaller players exit
- Stocks bouncing in extreme value territory

### 2025+: Stage 1 (Starvation, new cycle building)
- Capacity normalised
- Survivors slowly improving margins
- M&A activity (consolidators acquiring distressed)
- The set-up for the NEXT cycle

**Lesson**: A capital cycle trader would have:
- Entered TOPGLOV in early 2020 (Stage 2 start)
- Trimmed in mid-2020 (huge gains)
- EXITED ALL by end-Q4 2020 / Q1 2021 (Stage 3 warning)
- AVOIDED the sector entirely 2021-2024
- Beginning to nibble in 2025-26 (Stage 1)

Compare to most retail traders who bought heavily in late 2020 / early 2021 at the top and lost 70%+.

## Part 7 — Chancellor's Two-Question Test

Before any sector investment, ask:

```
Q1: Has the industry had high returns recently?
    → YES = late cycle warning
    → NO = potential opportunity

Q2: Has industry capex been rising sharply?
    → YES = supply shock coming
    → NO = setup favourable
```

**The 4-Quadrant Decision Matrix**:

| Recent Returns | Capex Growth | Implication |
|---------------|--------------|-------------|
| Low (-) | Falling (-) | **Stage 1 — Best entry** |
| Low (-) | Rising (+) | Avoid — premature; new entrants undermining recovery |
| High (+) | Falling (-) | **Stage 2 — Momentum window** |
| High (+) | Rising (+) | **Avoid — late cycle** |

The most counterintuitive insight: high-returning industries with high capex are the WORST investments going forward. Yet they're what gets the most positive press and analyst coverage.

## Part 8 — Combining Chancellor with Dalio

Dalio analyses the broad economic cycle. Chancellor analyses INDUSTRY-LEVEL supply cycles. They operate at different layers and should be COMBINED.

```
Layer 1 (Dalio): Where is the broader economic cycle?
                 → Phase 1, 2, 3, or 4?

Layer 2 (Chancellor): For each sector, where is the capital cycle?
                      → Stage 1, 2, 3, 4, or 5?
```

### The Optimal Combinations

```
Dalio Phase 1 + Chancellor Stage 1 = STRONGEST opportunity (early macro + early supply)
  Examples (KLSE 2026): Banking (Phase 1) + Plantation (Stage 1) overlap

Dalio Phase 1 + Chancellor Stage 2 = STRONG (recovery + supply still tight)
  Examples: Banking + Oil & Gas

Dalio Phase 2 + Chancellor Stage 3 = WARNING (mature macro + capex surge)
  Examples: Hot sectors with new entrants flooding in

Dalio Phase 3 + Chancellor Stage 4 = AVOID ABSOLUTELY
  Examples: Once-hot sectors now in overcapacity decline
```

### When the Two Frameworks Disagree

- Dalio says "Phase 1, recovery" but Chancellor says "Stage 4, overcapacity" for sector X → AVOID sector X even though macro is favourable
- Dalio says "Phase 3, defensive" but Chancellor says "Stage 2, boom" for sector Y → Selective opportunity (e.g., commodities in stagflation)

**Trust the industry-specific Chancellor signal more than the broad macro Dalio signal** for individual sector positioning.

## Part 9 — The Capital Cycle Anti-Patterns

### Anti-Pattern 1: Chasing the Hot Sector
The sector everyone's talking about is in Stage 3. By the time you've heard it, capex has been announced and the bust is 18-36 months out. AVOID.

### Anti-Pattern 2: "It's Cheap Now" After 50% Drop
A sector down 50% from peak is often in Stage 4 — STILL EARLY in the decline. Don't catch the falling knife.

### Anti-Pattern 3: "Demand is So Strong"
Demand analysis is irrelevant to cycle position. Plantation demand is always growing — but the price cycle is supply-driven.

### Anti-Pattern 4: "Analysts Are Bullish"
Analysts are MOST bullish in Stage 3 (just before the top). Their bullishness is itself the warning.

### Anti-Pattern 5: "The Industry Has Pricing Power"
Pricing power exists in Stage 1-2. By Stage 3 (capacity expansion), pricing power is about to evaporate. The current pricing power is the last sign of the late-cycle.

## Part 10 — The Capital Cycle Trading Routine

### Annual Sector Review (January, 4 hours)

For each KLSE sector:

```
1. Industry capex trajectory (last 5 years):
   - Capex YoY trend
   - Total capex vs historical average
   - Capex announcement pipeline

2. Industry returns trajectory:
   - Aggregate sector ROIC, ROE
   - Margin trend
   - Pricing trend (where applicable)

3. Industry behaviour:
   - M&A activity (defensive vs aggressive)
   - IPO/SPAC activity
   - Analyst coverage sentiment

4. Classify each sector to Stage 1-5:
   - Update sector cycle map

5. Identify:
   - Stage 1-2 sectors → HUNT
   - Stage 3 sectors → WARNING (reduce exposure)
   - Stage 4-5 sectors → AVOID
```

### Quarterly Update (Each quarter, 60 min)

```
1. Re-check sector capex from latest quarterlies
2. Note any new announcements moving sectors between stages
3. Update sector cycle map
4. Rebalance accordingly
```

### Monthly Tracking

```
Quick check: any sector M&A or major capex announcement that suggests stage change?
If yes → drill down
If no → maintain current stage classifications
```

## Part 11 — The 10 Chancellor Commandments

1. **Watch supply, not demand.** Capex announcements predict the future better than demand forecasts.

2. **High returns invite competition.** Today's profitable sector is tomorrow's overcrowded one.

3. **Low returns starve capacity.** Today's hated sector is being prepared for tomorrow's recovery.

4. **Capex surge = future supply shock = future price collapse.** Always.

5. **The 2-3 year lag.** Capex announced in 2024 produces capacity in 2026-2027. Position 2-3 years ahead.

6. **Universal bullishness is the late-cycle signal.** When everyone agrees, the cycle is mature.

7. **Cycle position matters more than valuation.** A cheap stock in Stage 4 will get cheaper. An expensive stock in Stage 2 can still double.

8. **Trust the industry survivors.** Companies that survived Stage 4-5 of the last cycle have low costs and pricing power — they win the next Stage 2.

9. **M&A patterns reveal the stage.** Defensive M&A = Stage 1; aggressive expansion M&A = Stage 3.

10. **Patience is the edge.** Capital cycles take 5-15 years. The trader willing to wait through Stage 4-5 captures Stage 2's full move.
