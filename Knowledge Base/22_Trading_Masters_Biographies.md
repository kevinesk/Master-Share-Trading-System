# Trading Masters — What Made Them Great & What You Can Copy

## Why Study the Masters?

Each master built their edge in a specific market environment with a specific approach. Study the PRINCIPLE behind their success, not just the technique. The technique changes; the principle endures.

---

## 1. Warren Buffett — The Compounder

**Born**: 1930, Omaha, Nebraska
**Peak AUM**: >$900 billion (Berkshire Hathaway)
**Approach**: Value investing; buy excellent businesses at fair prices; hold forever

### The Core Principle
> "It's far better to buy a wonderful company at a fair price than a fair company at a wonderful price."

**His edge**: Patience + Moat identification + Capital allocation discipline

### How He Picks Stocks (The Buffett Framework)

1. **Durable competitive advantage (economic moat)**
   - Brands (Coca-Cola, Apple) — pricing power
   - Network effects (Visa, American Express) — harder to replace
   - Cost advantages (GEICO insurance) — structural cost edge
   - Switching costs (See's Candies customer loyalty) — retention

2. **Simple, understandable business**
   - If you can't explain what they do in 2 sentences, skip it
   - He missed Google, Amazon, Microsoft for years — admitted it was outside his "circle of competence"

3. **Honest, capable management**
   - Management that treats shareholders like partners
   - Avoids companies with executives who pay themselves excessively

4. **Attractive price relative to intrinsic value**
   - He uses Owner Earnings: Net Income + Depreciation − Maintenance Capex
   - PE ratio alone is not sufficient — must assess 10-year earnings trajectory

### Metrics Buffett Looks For
| Metric | Buffett's Threshold |
|--------|-------------------|
| ROE | >15% consistently |
| Debt-to-equity | <0.5 (prefers no debt) |
| Net profit margin | Stable or expanding |
| Free cash flow | Consistently positive |
| Price-to-intrinsic value | ≤ 70% of true value |

### What KLSE Traders Can Copy
- Focus on businesses with pricing power — NESTLE, MAXIS, PETDAG
- Hold quality stocks through market volatility if thesis is intact
- Never overpay — even great businesses are bad investments at the wrong price
- Read annual reports every year — Buffett reads 500 pages/week

### What NOT to copy
- His holding period is literally "forever" — you don't have his tax advantages or capital base
- His concentration (40%+ in Apple) requires certainty most traders don't have

## 2. Mark Minervini — The Trend Trader

**Born**: 1962, New York
**Achievement**: US Investing Championship winner (155% return in 1997, 334% in 2021)
**Approach**: SEPA — Specific Entry Point Analysis; momentum + fundamentals

### The Core Principle
> "Make money in bull markets, lose nothing in bear markets."

**His edge**: Precision entry + Strict stop losses + Position sizing discipline

### What Makes Minervini Different

1. **Specific Entry Point**: He doesn't just find good stocks — he waits for the EXACT right moment to enter
2. **VCP Pattern**: Volatility Contraction Pattern — the signature setup covered in file 04
3. **Risk-first thinking**: Every trade starts with "how much can I lose?" not "how much can I make?"
4. **Superperformance**: Only trades stocks with characteristics of the biggest market winners in history

### The Superperformance Criteria
- EPS growth ≥70th percentile (top 30% of all stocks)
- Revenue growing as fast or faster than earnings
- RS Rating above 80 (top 20% of all stocks)
- Price at or near 52-week highs in Stage 2 uptrend
- VCP base with volume contraction

### What KLSE Traders Can Copy
- The entire SEPA/VCP system — it works on KLSE (files 02, 04, 12 in this knowledge base)
- His stop loss discipline: 7–8% maximum from entry
- His journal habit: He tracked every trade for years before becoming profitable
- His focus: He trades only his highest-conviction setups (4–8 stocks at a time)

## 3. Ray Dalio — The Macro Master

**Born**: 1949, New York
**AUM**: Bridgewater Associates — world's largest hedge fund (~$150B)
**Approach**: All Weather Portfolio + Pure Alpha; macro-driven systematic trading

### The Core Principle
> "He who lives by the crystal ball will eat shattered glass."

**His edge**: Risk parity + Understanding economic machines + Radical transparency

### The Economic Machine (Dalio's Framework)

Dalio views the economy as a machine with 3 moving parts:
1. **Productivity growth** (long-term, gradual, 1–2% per year)
2. **Short-term debt cycle** (5–8 years — business cycle)
3. **Long-term debt cycle** (75–100 years — generational)

**Key insight**: Most market crises are caused by debt cycle peaks, not productivity issues.

### The All Weather Portfolio (for any economic environment)

| Asset | Allocation | Environment It Thrives In |
|-------|-----------|--------------------------|
| Equities | 30% | Growth + low inflation |
| Long-term bonds | 40% | Falling growth or falling inflation |
| Gold | 7.5% | Rising inflation OR crisis |
| Commodities | 7.5% | Rising growth + rising inflation |
| Medium bonds | 15% | Deflation |

**KLSE adaptation**: In a rising inflation environment (like 2022), hold: TENAGA (utility pricing power), plantation stocks (commodity), gold-linked ETFs.

### What KLSE Traders Can Copy
- Think about the macro environment before picking stocks
- Diversify across assets that perform well in DIFFERENT environments
- Study his "How the Economic Machine Works" (30-min YouTube video — highly recommended)
- Prepare for downturns — always ask "what am I missing? Where am I wrong?"

### His Principle: Radical Open-Mindedness
- Actively seek evidence that you're wrong
- Rate your confidence in every belief (1–10)
- Be willing to change your view when evidence changes

## 4. George Soros — The Reflexivity Trader

**Born**: 1930, Budapest
**Peak AUM**: Quantum Fund — ~$45B
**Famous trade**: Short the British Pound in 1992 — made £1 billion in one day

### The Core Principle
> "It's not whether you're right or wrong that's important, but how much money you make when you're right and how much you lose when you're wrong."

**His edge**: Understanding reflexivity + Willingness to bet very large when conviction is high

### The Theory of Reflexivity

Soros believes markets are NOT efficient. Instead:
- **Perceptions change reality**: When enough investors believe a currency is weak, they sell it → it becomes weak
- **Bubbles are predictable**: The self-reinforcing cycle (rising prices → more buyers → rising prices) is exploitable
- **Know when to exit**: Bubbles eventually always pop. Exit before the crowd.

### What KLSE Traders Can Copy
- When a sector narrative takes hold and is self-reinforcing (data centres, AI, gloves during COVID), ride it but set a clear exit plan
- Size up when conviction is highest (Soros bet 100% of his fund on the pound trade)
- Recognise bubble conditions and prepare to exit aggressively

## 5. Peter Lynch — The Bottom-Up Investor

**Born**: 1944, Massachusetts
**Achievement**: Managed Fidelity Magellan Fund — 29% annual returns 1977–1990

### The Core Principle
> "Invest in what you know. Observe the world around you for investment ideas."

**His edge**: Consumer insight + Tireless company research + Simple logic

### Lynch's 6 Stock Categories
1. **Slow growers** (stalwarts): 2–4% growth; defensive; hold for dividend
2. **Stalwarts**: 10–12% growth; reliable; NESTLE, MAXIS on KLSE
3. **Fast growers**: 20–25% growth; highest return potential; VITROX type
4. **Cyclicals**: Up and down with economy; buy at cycle bottom; plantations on KLSE
5. **Turnarounds**: Near-bankruptcy to recovery; high risk, high reward
6. **Asset plays**: Hidden assets on balance sheet (land banks, investment properties)

### The "Tenbagger" Criteria (10× return stocks)
- Small company in a big industry (room to grow)
- Product/service that won't go out of fashion
- Company can be copied nationally/globally
- Low institutional ownership (not yet discovered)
- Management owns shares (aligned with shareholders)

### What KLSE Traders Can Copy
- Look for products and services you use daily — if they're great and the company is cheap, it's worth researching
- For KLSE: Observe which shopping malls are always packed (PAVREIT), which clinics have queues (KPJ), which apps everyone uses (99SMART)
- Read earnings reports quarterly; 15 minutes per stock is enough to track progress

## 6. Jesse Livermore — The Original Tape Reader

**Born**: 1877, Massachusetts
**Achievement**: Made $100M in the 1929 crash (equivalent to $1.5B today)
**Approach**: Pure price action + Pivotal points + Trend following

### The Core Principle
> "The market is never wrong — opinions often are."

**His edge**: Patience to wait for the right moment + Ruthless stop losses

### Livermore's Rules

1. **Only trade when conditions are right** — sit on cash 80% of the time
2. **Follow the line of least resistance** — trade with the trend, never against it
3. **Pivotal points** — buy stocks making new highs after a long consolidation (the VCP equivalent)
4. **Pyramiding** — add to winning positions, never to losing ones
5. **Cut losses immediately** — he was famous for exiting instantly when wrong

### What KLSE Traders Can Copy
- Patience: Most money is made waiting, not trading. Only trade the BEST setups.
- Livermore's "Pivotal Points" are essentially breakouts from long bases — exactly what VCP/Cup & Handle teach us
- Never average down: He went bankrupt several times by breaking this rule, then rebuilt by following it

## 7. BlackRock / Larry Fink — The Institutional Framework

**Founded**: 1988 by Larry Fink
**AUM**: >$10 trillion (world's largest asset manager)
**Approach**: Factor-based, risk-managed, quantitative investing

### What BlackRock Does Differently

1. **Factor investing**: Systematically buys stocks with proven factors — Value, Momentum, Quality, Low Volatility, Small Size
2. **Risk management first**: Every position is sized based on its contribution to portfolio risk (not just capital weight)
3. **ESG integration**: Environmental, Social, Governance factors are part of every investment decision
4. **Scale**: They own 5–8% of almost every major public company globally

### The 5 Factors BlackRock Uses (applicable to KLSE)

| Factor | Definition | KLSE Application |
|--------|-----------|-----------------|
| **Value** | Low PE, low P/B, high dividend yield | Screen for PE <15, DY >4% |
| **Momentum** | Strong recent price performance (RS Rating) | RS Rating >+10% vs KLCI |
| **Quality** | High ROE, stable earnings, low debt | ROE >15%, consistent EPS growth |
| **Low Volatility** | Stocks with stable price history | Utilities, telcos, REITs |
| **Small Size** | Small-cap premium | Mid-cap KLSE stocks outperform large-cap long-term |

### What KLSE Traders Can Copy
- Multi-factor screening: The fundamentals screener (file 03 in our system) already scores quality factors
- Combine momentum (RS Rating, TT Score) with quality (ROE, EPS growth) for the highest-probability setups
- Think in terms of risk, not just return: "How much can I lose?" is BlackRock's first question on every investment

## 8. Local Institutional Investor Mindset (EPF, PNB, Khazanah)

**They are NOT active traders.** They are long-term, fundamental, income-focused investors.

**EPF mandate**: Preserve capital, generate 6%+ gross dividend, invest >50% in Malaysia
**PNB (Permodalan Nasional Berhad) mandate**: Generate returns for Bumiputera unitholders
**Khazanah**: Strategic investments; national development; not purely profit-driven

**What they own**: The largest positions in MAYBANK, CIMB, TENAGA, AXIATA, IHH
**How they trade**: They DON'T — they accumulate over months/years, rarely sell

**What this means for KLSE retail traders**:
- Large institutional positions create price floors in quality stocks
- When EPF is a 10%+ shareholder, the stock has a long-term buyer who absorbs dips
- Track institutional shareholding changes via Bursa substantial shareholder disclosures
