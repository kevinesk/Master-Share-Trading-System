# Economic Cycles & Market Timing

## The Business Cycle — The Master Framework

All economies move in a recurring cycle. The same pattern has repeated for 200 years. Understanding where we are in the cycle determines which sectors to own and which to avoid.

```
         PEAK
        /     \
       /        \
EXPANSION        CONTRACTION
     /              \
    /                \
TROUGH/BOTTOM    → Next cycle begins
```

**Full cycle duration**: Typically 5–10 years (shorter cycles exist within larger super-cycles)

---

## The 4 Phases and What to Own

### Phase 1: Early Recovery (Trough → Early Expansion)

**Economic conditions**:
- GDP growth recovering from recession (turning from negative to positive)
- Central bank cuts rates (BNM and US Fed)
- Unemployment at peak but beginning to fall
- Consumer confidence starting to improve
- Credit conditions loosening

**Market behaviour**:
- Stocks bottom BEFORE the economy (markets are forward-looking by 6–9 months)
- First rally often doubted ("it's just a dead cat bounce")
- Small caps and cyclicals outperform

**KLSE sectors to BUY**:
- ✅ Banking (first beneficiary of rate cuts + lending recovery)
- ✅ Property (rate cuts make mortgages cheap)
- ✅ Consumer discretionary (MRDIY, retail)
- ✅ Technology (pent-up corporate IT spending)
- ✅ Industrials / construction (infrastructure projects restart)

**KLSE sectors to AVOID**:
- ❌ Utilities (already priced in as safe haven; others catch up)
- ❌ Telcos (defensive; underperform in risk-on)

---

### Phase 2: Mid-Cycle Expansion (The Best Time to Be Invested)

**Economic conditions**:
- GDP growth strong and accelerating (Malaysia: 5–6%+ GDP)
- Employment rising, wages increasing
- Corporate earnings growing 15–25%+ YoY
- Central bank holds rates (or begins hiking slowly)
- Investor confidence high but not euphoric

**Market behaviour**:
- Strong bull market; most stocks participating
- "All boats rise" — even mediocre stocks gain
- Volume expanding; breadth wide

**KLSE sectors to BUY** (all sectors do well, but leaders):
- ✅ Technology / Semiconductors (capex boom)
- ✅ Banking (loan growth + NIM stable)
- ✅ Consumer discretionary (wages up, spending up)
- ✅ REITs (mall traffic recovering; offices filling)
- ✅ Industrials (capex investment everywhere)

**Strategy**: This is the phase to be fully invested. Use full position sizing. Let winners run. VCP and Cup & Handle setups work extremely well here.

---

### Phase 3: Late Cycle (Peak)

**Economic conditions**:
- GDP growth still positive but decelerating
- Inflation rising (CPI >3–4%)
- Central bank hiking rates aggressively (fighting inflation)
- Labour market tight; wages rising fast → cost pressure on companies
- Credit conditions tightening

**Market behaviour**:
- Stocks at all-time highs but breadth narrowing (only a few sectors still rising)
- "Distribution" patterns forming on weekly charts
- Small caps and cyclicals beginning to underperform
- Market increasingly volatile — sharp rallies and sharp drops

**KLSE sectors to BUY** (defensive rotation):
- ✅ Energy / Commodities (inflation hedge — CPO, oil)
- ✅ Materials (pricing power — MCEMENT if prices rising)
- ✅ Utilities (stable earnings regardless of economy)
- ✅ Telcos (defensive dividend yield)

**KLSE sectors to START REDUCING**:
- ⚠️ Technology (valuation stretched; first to fall in rate hikes)
- ⚠️ Property (higher mortgage rates kill demand)
- ⚠️ Consumer discretionary (wage growth offset by inflation)
- ⚠️ Banking (NIM peaks; credit risk rising)

**Key signal**: When fewer and fewer stocks are making new 52-week highs even as the index makes new highs → distribution is occurring. Reduce exposure.

---

### Phase 4: Recession / Contraction

**Economic conditions**:
- GDP negative for 2+ consecutive quarters (recession)
- Unemployment rising sharply
- Corporate earnings falling; dividend cuts
- Central bank pivoting to rate cuts (too late)
- Credit crises possible (banks restrict lending)

**Market behaviour**:
- Bear market; most stocks falling
- "Value traps" — stocks look cheap but keep falling
- KLCI below EMA200; EMA50 crossing below EMA200 ("Death Cross")

**KLSE sectors to HOLD** (defensive):
- ✅ Utilities (TENAGA — people still pay electricity bills)
- ✅ Healthcare (IHH, KPJ — healthcare demand stable)
- ✅ Consumer staples (NESTLE, QL — food is non-discretionary)
- ✅ Telcos (MAXIS, CDB — essential services)
- ✅ CASH — most important holding in Phase 4

**KLSE sectors AVOID entirely**:
- ❌ Banking (NPL rising; earnings falling)
- ❌ Property (transactions collapsing)
- ❌ Technology (capex freezes)
- ❌ Industrials (no new projects)

## Leading vs Lagging Indicators

**Leading indicators** (change BEFORE the economy):
- Stock market (6–9 months ahead)
- Building permits / construction approvals
- New orders in manufacturing (PMI new orders sub-index)
- Consumer confidence surveys
- Money supply growth (M2)

**Lagging indicators** (change AFTER the economy):
- Unemployment rate (lags by 6–12 months)
- Bank NPL ratio (lags by 6–12 months)
- GDP (officially confirmed 45 days after quarter ends)
- CPI (lags; inflation is last to peak)

**Trading implication**: Don't wait for GDP confirmation before buying. The market has already priced in the recovery 6–9 months earlier. Look at LEADING indicators to anticipate where we're going.

## The Yield Curve — The Recession Predictor

**What it is**: The relationship between short-term and long-term US Treasury yields.

```
Normal yield curve (Phase 1 & 2):
Long-term yield > Short-term yield
→ Economy healthy; lend long-term with confidence

Inverted yield curve (Phase 3 & 4):
Short-term yield > Long-term yield
→ Recession signal; historical accuracy >90% with 12–18 month lag
```

**TradingView**: Plot `TVC:US10Y` minus `TVC:US02Y` on same chart. When the line goes NEGATIVE → recession warning within 12–18 months.

**KLSE implication**: US yield curve inversion → expect KLSE to peak 6–12 months later → begin reducing equity exposure when inversion occurs.

## The Kondratiev Wave (50–60 Year Super-Cycle)

While less actionable for short-term trading, understanding the Kondratiev Wave (K-Wave) explains the BIG picture:

```
K-Wave 1: 1800–1850 (Steam/textiles)
K-Wave 2: 1850–1900 (Steel/railways)
K-Wave 3: 1900–1950 (Electrification/chemicals)
K-Wave 4: 1950–2000 (Petrochemicals/electronics)
K-Wave 5: 2000–present (Information technology/AI)
```

**We are likely in the expansion phase of K-Wave 5** — dominated by AI, semiconductors, data centres, clean energy. This explains why technology stocks globally (including Malaysian semiconductor names) have outsized opportunities in this decade.

**KLSE implication**: The data centre/AI theme in Johor and KL is not a short-term trend — it's aligned with the K-Wave 5 expansion. These structural positions (YTLPOWR, TENAGA, TM, GAMUDA) can be held for multi-year periods.
