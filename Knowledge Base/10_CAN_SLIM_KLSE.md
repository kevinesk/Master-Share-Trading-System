# William O'Neil CAN SLIM — Adapted for KLSE

## What is CAN SLIM?

CAN SLIM is William O'Neil's growth stock trading system, developed by studying every major winning stock from 1880–2009. Each letter represents a key characteristic of stocks that went on to make 100–1000% gains.

> "The whole secret to winning big in the stock market is not to be right all the time, but to lose the least amount possible when you're wrong."
> — William O'Neil

**KLSE adaptation**: The principles apply directly, but the thresholds are adjusted for the Malaysian market's lower average earnings growth rates.

---

## C — Current Quarterly Earnings

**O'Neil's rule**: EPS growth ≥25% in the most recent quarter vs same quarter last year.

**KLSE adaptation**:
- ≥20% EPS growth = strong (KLSE growth rates are lower than US)
- ≥15% EPS growth = acceptable if accelerating
- Negative EPS growth = avoid, regardless of other factors

**What to look for**:
- Acceleration: Q1 grew 10%, Q2 grew 15%, Q3 grew 22% → **accelerating is key**
- Ignore one-off boosts (tax credits, asset sales) — look at core operating earnings
- Revenue must also be growing (earnings without revenue growth = unsustainable)

**KLSE data sources**:
- Quarterly reports on Bursa (Financial Reports section)
- klsescreener.com → stock page → Quarterly tab
- i3investor.com for earnings history

**Red flags**:
- Earnings growth slowing for 2+ consecutive quarters
- Revenue declining while earnings up (cost cutting, not growth)
- Gross margin shrinking

## A — Annual Earnings Growth

**O'Neil's rule**: EPS growth ≥25% annually for at least 3 consecutive years.

**KLSE adaptation**:
- ≥15% annual EPS growth for 3 years = strong
- ≥10% for 5 years = acceptable for dividend/quality stocks
- Use 3Y and 5Y EPS CAGR from klsescreener fundamentals report

**Return on Equity**: Must be ≥17% (O'Neil's benchmark). KLSE threshold: ≥15%.

| Annual EPS CAGR | Rating | Action |
|----------------|--------|--------|
| ≥25% | Excellent | Core position |
| 15–24% | Good | Standard sizing |
| 10–14% | Acceptable | Smaller sizing, stronger technical required |
| <10% | Weak | Avoid for CAN SLIM plays |

## N — New Product, Service, Management, or High

**Three types of "New"**:

1. **New product/service**: A company launching something that changes its growth trajectory
   - KLSE examples: Data centre wins (YTL, Gamuda), EV charging rollout, AI integration
   - Clincher: New product = new earnings growth catalyst

2. **New management**: Turnaround story with new CEO who has a track record of results
   - Check: What did this person achieve at their last company?

3. **New high**: Stock making 52-week or all-time highs
   - Counterintuitive but proven: stocks making new highs tend to go higher
   - O'Neil: "Buy high, sell higher — buy low, sell even lower"
   - On KLSE: New 52-week high + volume = a powerful signal

**The New High Rule**: Do not fear stocks at 52-week highs. Fear stocks at 52-week lows.

## S — Supply and Demand (Shares Outstanding + Volume)

**Share Supply**:
- Prefer companies with ≤50 million shares outstanding (smaller float = easier to move)
- KLSE: Stocks with <200 million shares generally more responsive
- Beware of companies that have diluted shares frequently (rights issues, warrants conversions)

**Volume on Breakout**:
- At least 40–50% above average on breakout day (O'Neil)
- KLSE minimum: ≥1.5× 20-day average volume
- Institutional-quality breakout: ≥3× average volume

**Share buybacks**: Positive signal — management believes stock is undervalued. Check if company is buying back shares consistently.

**Free float consideration for KLSE**:
- Low free float (major shareholder holds >60%) = thin trading volume, wide spreads
- Watch for insider buying/selling via Bursa disclosures

## L — Leader or Laggard?

**Buy the market leader in a leading sector — not the second-best name.**

O'Neil's RS Rating system: The top 20% of stocks = leaders. Bottom 80% = laggards.
- Our RS Rating vs KLCI works the same way
- **Rule**: Only buy stocks with RS Rating ≥ +5% vs KLCI over 12 months

**How to identify the sector leader**:
1. Find the sector with strongest recent performance
2. Within that sector, find the stock with the highest RS Rating
3. That stock is your leader — trade it, not the second or third name

**KLSE sector leadership examples**:
- Banking rally: CIMB often leads, MAYBANK follows — buy CIMB
- Tech rally: VITROX often leads semiconductors — buy VITROX, not the smaller names
- Data centre: YTL POWER leads — ride the leader, not the followers

**Never buy laggards expecting them to "catch up"** — they lag for a reason.

## I — Institutional Sponsorship

**O'Neil's rule**: At least 3–5 reputable institutions must own the stock.

**KLSE equivalent**: Look for evidence of institutional buying:
- EPF increasing stake (disclosed via Bursa substantial shareholder notices)
- Foreign fund buying (show up in Bursa foreign flow data)
- PNB, Khazanah, KWAP, KWSP buying = strong institutional validation
- Analyst upgrades with STRONG BUY ratings from major brokers

**Check Bursa disclosures**:
- Go to Bursa website → Company Announcements → "Change in Substantial Shareholder"
- If EPF or major foreign fund is buying: follow them

**Red flags**:
- Heavy insider selling while price is at highs
- EPF reducing stake significantly
- All broker ratings are HOLD or SELL

## M — Market Direction

**The most important letter. All others are irrelevant if M is wrong.**

O'Neil's rule: 3 out of 4 stocks follow the general market direction. When the market is falling, even the best stocks fall.

**KLSE Market Direction Checklist**:

| Indicator | Bullish | Bearish |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| KLCI vs EMA50 | Above | Below |
| KLCI vs EMA200 | Above | Below |
| KLCI trend (50-day slope) | Rising | Falling |
| Dow Jones | Above EMA20 | Below EMA20 |
| KLCI McClellan Osc. | Positive (+50 to +150) | Negative |
| Distribution days in last 25 sessions | ≤4 | ≥5 |

**Distribution Day (O'Neil's key concept)**:
- KLCI falls >0.2% on volume HIGHER than the previous day
- 5+ distribution days in 25 sessions = market under distribution = sell and raise cash

**Follow-Through Day (confirmed bull market)**:
- After a low, KLCI rallies ≥1% on higher volume than previous day
- Must occur on Day 4 or later from the market low
- One follow-through day is not enough — look for clusters

## O'Neil's Key Rules Adapted for KLSE

1. **Cut losses at 7–8%** — no exceptions. O'Neil: "If you bought correctly, you shouldn't need more room."

2. **Never average down** — only average UP into a winner, never down into a loser.

3. **Don't buy in downtrending markets** — wait for a confirmed follow-through day.

4. **Sell when you have a 20–25% gain** in most stocks — let your very best hold longer.

5. **Keep your best 5–8 stocks** — a concentrated, high-quality portfolio outperforms a diversified one.

6. **Sell on the way UP** — don't wait for the stock to roll over before taking profits.

7. **You don't need to own stocks every day** — cash is a position when conditions are wrong.
